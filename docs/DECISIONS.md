# Decisions (ADR log)

Short records of choices that shaped this repo. Dated 2026-11-04. The base conventions in `CLAUDE.md` (DOM-only UI, one store whose actions own the log, shared resources) are taken as given and not repeated here.

## ADR-1: Constant-rate rotation model

**Context.** A real flip is driven by the front foot dragging off the kick, and its spin slows with air drag and changes as the board's inertia tensor wobbles. None of that is needed to teach "rotation rate times time in the air".
**Decision.** Flip and shove are constant angular rates for the whole airtime: `rollTotal = flick * T * 360`, `yawTotal = scoop * T * 360`, with `T = 2 * sqrt(2h / g)`. Height is the projectile parabola. Pitch (nose up 18 degrees easing to level by 35 percent of T) is cosmetic and not part of recognition. (Superseded in part by ADR-27 on 2026-11-04: the pop now pivots on the rear wheels to 32.5 degrees so the tail strikes the pad; still cosmetic.) (Superseded in part by ADR-36 on 2026-11-05: the totals and their formula stand, but the rate is phased: it ramps up after the tail strike, runs free and stops in a catch at 0.64 to 0.76.)
**Consequence.** The whole lesson is one multiplication the student can check against the readouts: more pop buys more time, so the same flick gives more rotation. The model is pure (`src/sim/flight.ts`) and tested to the brief's numbers.

## ADR-2: Euler order YXZ (superseded by ADR-32 on 2026-11-04)

**Decision.** The board group uses `rotation.set(roll, yaw, pitch, 'YXZ')`: yaw about world Y first, then roll about the board's own long axis (X), then pitch about its own Z.
**Consequence.** The flip axis follows the shove, which is what a varial or 360 flip looks like: the board spins flat and flips about its current long axis, not about a fixed world axis. With order XYZ a varial would wobble off-axis. Superseded: once the pop pitched the board (ADR-27) the pitch, applied last, swung the raised nose sideways while it rolled; ADR-32 moves the pitch before the roll.

## ADR-3: Board pose lives in a ref, not in React state

**Decision.** `FlightDriver` owns the only per-frame callback that writes the pose. It advances progress with the frame delta, writes `{ y, roll, yaw, pitch }` into one reused object with `writePose`, and applies it to the Board group and shadow disc. Board has no per-frame code.
**Consequence.** No React render per frame. The pose can never disagree with the timeline because both derive from the same progress number. Deviation from the brief's wording: the brief has "the Board group reads that ref"; here FlightDriver applies the ref to the Board group in the same callback, which avoids a second `useFrame` and any ordering question between the two.
**Amended (audit fix, 2026-11-04): the frame clock and the cursor.** The old clamp of every delta to 1/30 s is superseded: it guarded against the long first delta after the demand loop idled, but it also slowed any machine under 30 fps (at 15 fps a 1x trick played at 0.5x). The step now lives in `src/scene/flightClock.ts`, pure and tested with a real store:
- The first playing frame of a run (a pop, a resume, a remount of the driver after a tier change) advances by 0, whatever idle time its delta carries. A store subscription marks a pop or a resume as a new run, so a pause and resume (or a scrub, its release and a resume) that all fall between two frames still start cleanly.
- Every later frame advances by the real delta, capped at 0.1 s (`MAX_STALL`), so 15, 30 and 60 fps reach the same progress after the same time and a background tab, a GC pause or a 2 s stall moves the board one capped step, never to its landing.
- The cursor is the store's live playhead (`store.playhead`, ADR-4): the clock reads and advances it in place every frame, without allocating, and the actions that put the board somewhere on purpose (pop, pause, resume, scrub, reset, landing) write it. So pause before the first 10 Hz commit, a remount mid-flight and a scrub then resume all continue from where the board is, and an explicit destination always wins. A playhead left on another flight is ignored and replaced by the store's progress.
- The 10 Hz `syncProgress` commit counts advanced time and resets after each commit, so the store hears at most 10 commits a second at any frame rate; `land()` is still called once, at progress 1. Both stay store actions; the clock only reports which one is due.

## ADR-4: Progress commits to the store at 10 Hz

**Decision.** While playing, progress reaches the store at most every 100 ms (`syncProgress`, not logged). `land()` is called the frame progress reaches 1.
**Consequence.** The timeline thumb visibly steps during playback (DESIGN.md calls this honest), React re-renders about 5 times in a 0.53 s trick, and the scene description does not churn. `frameloop` is `"always"` only while `status === 'playing'`.

## ADR-5: Landing tolerances and preset values

**Decision.** Landed if the flip is within 30 degrees of a whole revolution and the shove within 25 degrees of a half turn. When a trick is not landed, the reported axis is the one furthest past its tolerance and the sign of its error gives under- or over-rotated. Presets keep the brief's values, which `tricks.test.ts` verifies land at pop 0.35 m and fail (under-rotated) at 0.15 m:

| Preset | Pop | Flick | Scoop | At 0.35 m (T 0.534 s) | At 0.15 m (T 0.350 s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kickflip | 0.35 | 1.9 | 0 | roll 365, landed | roll 239, under by 121 |
| Pop shove-it | 0.35 | 0 | 0.9 | yaw 173, landed | yaw 113, under by 67 |
| Varial flip | 0.35 | 1.9 | 0.9 | roll 365, yaw 173, landed | under on flip |
| 360 flip | 0.35 | 1.9 | 1.9 | roll 365, yaw 365, landed | under on flip |

No adjustment was needed. The bonus is reachable: pop 0.20 m with flick 2.5 gives 364 degrees.
**Consequence.** Shove is tighter than flip because a half-turned board landing 25 degrees off is already a sketchy landing, while a flip that is 30 degrees short still gets caught by the feet.

## ADR-6: Reduced motion means scrub-only, not slow

**Decision.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion` a pop resolves at once (one announcement with the inputs and the result), the board jumps to its final pose, and the timeline is enabled so the student can step through the flight. There is no auto-play at any speed. The camera has no easing in either mode.
**Consequence.** The lesson is intact (every pose is reachable, readouts are identical) with zero animated frames. The store holds `reducedMotion`, synced from the media query by `App`, so the keyboard shortcut and the button behave the same.
**Update (audit fix, 2026-11-04).** One rule, `animates(s) = !s.reducedMotion && !s.sceneUnavailable` (`src/store/playback.ts`, exported from the store), decides whether a pop plays. Turning reduced motion on while a flight is playing or paused now resolves it at once through the landing path (`land`), exactly once, in one announcement that opens "Reduced motion on: the board goes straight to its landing." and then reads the result and any objectives; repeating the preference or a late `land()` from the driver does nothing. Resume never sets `playing` when `animates` is false: it resolves the flight instead. The same rule covers a 3D view that cannot render: `SceneBoundary` (`src/ui/SceneBoundary.tsx`) wraps the scene's `Suspense`, so a rejected scene chunk or an error thrown by the renderer no longer unmounts the whole app. It calls `setSceneUnavailable` (ui slice, not persisted, not logged), which announces the failure once, resolves a trick in the air in that same announcement, and from then on every pop resolves at once as under reduced motion. The stage shows a plain note in its place (no new live region; the Announcer stays the only one), and the controls, the timeline, the readouts, the objectives and the export keep working without FlightDriver. Reset keeps the flag, because the view is still gone; a reload tries the scene again.

## ADR-7: Reset clears the score but keeps the log

**Decision.** `session.reset` restores default inputs, playback (idle, 1x, no flight) and clears objectives, but appends to the existing event log.
**Consequence.** A teacher reviewing the export sees earlier attempts. Event `t` stays relative to the original session start.

## ADR-8: Event log panel shows the latest 200 rows

**Context.** A log panel that renders every event forever grows the heap and the DOM slowly over a long session.
**Decision.** The panel renders `events.slice(-200)` and says when it is capped. The store and the JSON export keep every event.
**Consequence.** DOM size is bounded in long sessions; assessment data is never lost.

## ADR-9: Theme default dark, clear color per theme

**Decision.** `data-theme="dark"` is set in `index.html`; a saved choice in `localStorage` under `trick-lab.theme` is applied by an inline script before first paint. The scene clear color follows the theme (`SCENE_BG.dark` and `.light`), and `artDirection.test.ts` asserts each equals `--scene-bg` in the matching `tokens.css` block. Lights and materials do not change with the theme.
**Consequence.** A theme toggle costs one background color change and no shader recompile; the stage never flashes a mismatched color on resize.

## ADR-10: Rotation direction convention (integration pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** The sim produces unsigned totals (degrees of flip and shove); nothing said which way the board turns on screen, and the implementation rolled the grip toward the toe side, the opposite of the convention the lead asked for.
**Decision.** The rider is regular stance. The board's nose is +X, the toe side is +Z (the side facing the default camera), the heel side is -Z. Positive flick rolls the grip toward the heel side (toe edge up and over), the kickflip direction: a negative rotation about the board's +X. Positive scoop swings the tail behind the heels, clockwise seen from above, the backside shove-it direction: a negative rotation about world +Y. (The lead first specified counterclockwise; the integration pass flagged that backside means the tail swings behind the rider, and the lead corrected the sign on 2026-11-04.) The signs live in `ART.stance` (`artDirection.ts`) and are applied in one place, `setBoardRotation` (`scene/boardPose.ts`); the sim stays unsigned, so recognition is unchanged. All four presets share the convention: the varial flip is the kickflip roll plus the shove-it yaw, and the 360 flip doubles the yaw. The scene description says the convention in words.
**Consequence.** `boardPose.test.ts` checks the directions on the real Euler math (grip toward the heel side at 25 percent of a kickflip, graphic up at 50 percent, tail toward the heels and clockwise at 25 percent of a shove-it, the same senses for varial and 360 flips). Goofy stance would be a sign flip on both axes in `ART.stance`; it is not offered.

## ADR-11: Fit the whole pad and the highest pop, not a fixed width (integration pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** At 1440 x 900 the stage is 784 x 876 px (taller than wide) and at 1024 x 768 it is 368 x 744 px. The DESIGN.md pad (3.6 x 2.8 m) overflowed the stage on every side, and fitting it whole would need a camera 15 m away at 1024 x 768, with the board a few pixels long.
**Decision.** The pad is 1.6 x 1.2 m (two deck lengths, wider than the 0.84 m circle the deck sweeps in a shove). `scene/cameraFit.ts` computes, in closed form, the smallest distance along the home view direction at which the pad's eight corners and a box around the board at a 0.6 m pop fit the frustum with a 5 percent margin; `SceneCamera` applies it on every resize and on Reset view, and lets OrbitControls zoom out to 1.25 times the fit even past the design's 4.5 m.
**Consequence.** Pad and full arc are in frame at 1440 x 900, 1024 x 768 and the stacked 900 px layout. On the narrow 1024 x 768 stage the board is about 110 px long; a mid-width layout that gives the stage more room is a follow-up.

## ADR-12: The realistic board is a scripted GLB, not more JSX primitives (realistic board pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** The board had to look like a real skateboard, and the goal was a real glTF/GLB asset pipeline with budgets, compression and disposal. More primitives in JSX would have looked better but shown none of that. There is no 3D artist on this project.
**Decision.** The board is a procedural asset built by code and shipped as a compressed GLB. `src/asset/` holds the geometry as pure modules: the deck outline, kicks, concave and nested-ring deck mesh in `deck.ts`; trucks, bolts and the wheel lathe in `hardware.ts`; deterministic textures in `textures.ts`; every number from DESIGN.md section 7 in `spec.ts`. `npm run assets` (`scripts/build-board.ts`) turns them into a glTF-Transform document: 7 meshes, one per material, and 4 PNG textures (seeded grip grit, ply stripes, wheel bands, and the deck graphic rasterized from `assets/source/deck-graphic.svg` with resvg). It then applies meshopt compression and quantization and writes `public/models/board.glb`, which is committed. `npm run assets:check` (`scripts/check-assets.ts`) reads the committed file back and fails on any budget miss; it is the last step of `npm run verify`. The runtime loads the GLB with drei `useGLTF`, preloads it, and renders the cached nodes with `dispose={null}`. A RoomEnvironment PMREM, made once per Canvas mount and disposed on unmount, gives the metals something to reflect.
**Consequence.** The asset is reviewable as code. The tests check real properties: length and width within 2 mm, kick angles, 12 mm concave, z symmetry, outward winding, wheel bottoms on the pad, UV orientation, triangle counts, deterministic bytes, and that the committed GLB matches the geometry source. It is honestly a procedural, scripted asset: no sculpting, no baked lighting, no normal maps, and a modeller would do the trucks better. The artist path (a Blender export through the same `check-assets` gate) is in `docs/ASSET-PIPELINE.md`.

## ADR-13: Meshopt over Draco (realistic board pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** Both shrink geometry. drei's `useGLTF` turns Draco on by default and points its decoder at a Google CDN (`gstatic.com`), fetched at runtime as a separate WASM file. School Chromebooks often sit behind filters that block CDNs, and the app should work offline once loaded.
**Decision.** Geometry uses `EXT_meshopt_compression` with `KHR_mesh_quantization` (14-bit positions, 8-bit octahedral normals, 12-bit UVs, through glTF-Transform's `meshopt()` at level `high`). The runtime calls `useGLTF(url, false, true)`: Draco off, meshopt on. The meshopt decoder ships inside the JS bundle (three-stdlib), so there is no extra request and nothing to self-host. `check-assets` fails if Draco appears.
**Consequence.** Geometry is about 88 KB of the 250 KB GLB for 13,904 triangles. Meshopt decodes faster than Draco, and its output still compresses well under gzip or brotli on the server. Draco can compress dense meshes further, which matters for scanned or sculpted assets, not for this one. If Draco is ever needed, self-host its decoder (`useGLTF.setDecoderPath('/draco/')`).

## ADR-14: KTX2 textures deferred, PNG inside the GLB for now (realistic board pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** KTX2 with Basis Universal keeps textures compressed on the GPU, which is the real memory win on a 4 GB Chromebook. Encoding needs the native `toktx` binary (KTX-Software), and this pass was not allowed to install system tools.
**Decision.** Ship the four textures as PNG inside the GLB: 2.70 MB of GPU memory estimated (RGBA8 plus mips), within budget. The grip is a one-channel PNG: the recipe moves all three channels together, and the material's base colour factor restores the blue lean. That took the GLB from 309 KB to 250 KB. KTX2 is the next step, once `toktx` is on the build machine:

```
npx @gltf-transform/cli@4.5.1 uastc public/models/board.glb public/models/board.glb --slots "baseColorTexture" --level 2 --zstd 18
```

or per texture, with KTX-Software directly:

```
toktx --t2 --encode uastc --uastc_quality 2 --zcmp 18 --genmipmap --assign_oetf srgb deck-bottom.ktx2 deck-bottom.png
```

The runtime then needs a `KTX2Loader` with a self-hosted Basis transcoder. Copy `three/examples/jsm/libs/basis/` to `public/basis/` and pass it through `useGLTF`'s `extendLoader` argument with `ktx2Loader.setTranscoderPath('/basis/').detectSupport(gl)`. `check-assets` should then require `KHR_texture_basisu`.
**Consequence.** UASTC transcodes to BC7 or ASTC 4x4 at 1 byte per pixel, so the estimate would drop from about 2.7 MB to about 0.7 MB. ETC1S would go lower, at some cost in quality on the grip grain. The cost is a transcoder download (a WASM file plus its JS) and a build step that depends on a native binary. Not measured yet.

## ADR-15: Board detail as the LOD and the fallback (realistic board pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** The GLB board costs 7 draw calls and about 13,900 triangles. That is fine on a desktop GPU and probably fine on a Chromebook, but nobody has measured one yet (PERF-BUDGET.md). The model also has to stream before it can render, and it can fail to load.
**Decision.** One component tree, two levels. The primitive board from DESIGN.md section 2 (renamed `BoardPrimitive.tsx`: 5 draw calls, 436 triangles, no textures) serves three roles: the Suspense fallback while the GLB streams, the error-boundary fallback if it fails, and the "Low" level of a "Board detail" setting in the View panel (a native radio group, DESIGN.md 7.7). The setting lives in the ui slice, defaults to High, persists under `trick-lab.board-detail` like the theme, announces changes, and is never logged (the event contract has no UI events). If Low was chosen before the scene loaded, the GLB is never downloaded: the GLTF loader and decoder are a separate 72 KB chunk (`BoardModel`), and the preload is skipped. If loading fails, the store records it, the control is disabled showing Low, and the announcer says so. FlightDriver poses the outer group, so both levels move identically.
**Update (audit fix, 2026-11-04).** The tier in use is `effectiveQuality(s)` (`src/store/quality.ts`, exported from the store): Low once a model failed, otherwise the saved or auto-picked `quality`. The View panel reads it, and every scene consumer of the tier (renderer settings, board, feet, ground) is to read it too, so a failed model can no longer show "Low" while High renderer settings stay on. The saved `quality` and `trick-lab.quality` are left untouched, so the student's choice returns on the next load.
**Consequence.** A slow machine has a one-click escape hatch back to the original scene cost (9 scene draw calls and 436 triangles, measured), and a blocked network still gets a working lab. It is a manual LOD, not distance-based: the board is the only object and is always about the same size on screen, so switching on distance would buy nothing. Switching to Low does not free the GLB's GPU memory once it has loaded (the cache keeps it, `dispose={null}`). Releasing it would take `useGLTF.clear(url)` plus disposing its geometries and textures, a follow-up if memory, not draw calls, turns out to be the Chromebook limit.

## ADR-16: Quality tiers, desktop as the default target (quality pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** The brief targeted low-end Chromebooks, so the renderer ran without antialiasing, with DPR capped at 1.5, no anisotropic filtering and a flat shadow disc. On a desktop (RTX 3060, the lead's machine) the board read as pixelated. The lead changed the target: the default is a desktop with a real GPU, with fallbacks for Chromebooks.
**Decision.** Three tiers in the ui slice (`quality: 'high' | 'medium' | 'low'`, persisted under `trick-lab.quality`, announced, never logged). High: MSAA, DPR up to 2, 16x anisotropy, real shadows, the high-detail board GLB and the shoe GLB. Medium: MSAA, DPR 1.5, 4x anisotropy, the original board GLB, no shadows, primitive shoes. Low: the original Chromebook setup (no MSAA, DPR 1.5, the primitive board and shoes, the shadow disc). The first visit picks a tier from the device (`src/store/quality.ts`: a software renderer, a Chromebook user agent, 4 GB or 2 cores give Low; a known desktop GPU or 8 GB with 8 cores gives High; phones and tablets and everything else give Medium), and the View panel's hint says "Picked for this device" until the student chooses. A legacy saved "Board detail: Low" maps to Low. The Canvas is keyed on the tier because antialias and powerPreference are WebGL context options; the GLB caches survive the remount. Every number per tier is one table in `scene/artDirection.ts` (`QUALITY`).
**Consequence.** Chromebooks keep exactly the scene cost measured before (9 draw calls, 436 triangles) and never download a model. Desktops get the look the lead asked for. The per-asset budgets in `src/asset/spec.ts` (`BUDGETS`) are gates per tier: the Medium board keeps its 300 KB, 8 calls, 25,000 triangles, 4 textures at 1024 px; the High board gets 2.5 MB, 8 calls, 120,000 triangles, 6 textures at 2048 px; the shoes 600 KB, 3 calls, 30,000 triangles, 2 textures. The whole-scene draw-call limit is 20 on Low and Medium and 40 on High, where `renderer.info` also counts the shadow pass. A real Chromebook run is still owed (PERF-BUDGET.md).

## ADR-17: Feet from the inputs, with a bail (quality pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** The lead asked for shoes that move around the board the way a skater's feet would, and chose that they follow the student's inputs (pop, flick, scoop) rather than the recognized trick, so a wrong setting shows a wrong foot motion.
**Decision.** A pure feet model, `src/sim/feet.ts`, maps the inputs and the flight progress to two foot poses in the board's rest frame (positions, a yaw about Y and a pitch about the shoe's own Z). The feet do not rotate with the board: the stance before the pop comes from the inputs (the front foot turns toward the nose and hangs its heel over the heel-side edge as the flick grows; the back foot's toes move toward the toe-side pocket as the scoop grows), then the pop, scoop drag, slide, flick, hover and gather phases scale with the inputs, and the feet come down over the bolts. Whether they land on the board or bail comes from the same tolerance rule recognition uses (`isLanded`, allocation-free): a failed flight has the feet step off to the heel side and land on the pad from 62 percent of the airtime. FlightDriver remains the only per-frame writer and poses the two shoe groups beside the board; the store state doubles as the inputs object, so the per-frame path allocates nothing. The shoes are a "Show feet" toggle in the View panel (persisted, announced, never logged), and `describeScene` says where the feet are for screen-reader users.
**Consequence.** Moving a slider moves the stance at once, which is the teaching cue. The model is a readable approximation, not biomechanics: the feet hover a fixed 12 cm above the board at mid-flight, and the bail ignores the board's roll and yaw (the feet step to fixed heel-side spots in the rest frame, so a board that stopped at a quarter turn can overlap a shoe). The front foot is the left shoe, mirrored at runtime from the one right shoe in `shoes.glb`, so the stance is regular only; goofy would need the mirror and the signs in ADR-10 flipped together. (The fixed hover and the gather over the bolts are superseded by ADR-36 on 2026-11-05: a per-foot tuck and a catch timed to the board.)

## ADR-18: Frame the flight, not the pad (quality pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** ADR-11 fit the whole 1.6 x 1.2 m pad plus the highest pop, which put the camera 3.5 m away at 1440 x 900 and left the board about 300 px long: a large part of the "pixelated" look.
**Decision.** `framingPoints` now takes only a box around the flight: the deck's spin circle from the pad surface under the board (so the shadow stays in frame) to the highest pop. The pad may run off the edges. `minDistance` drops from 1.6 to 1.0 m so the fit can get close on wide stages; Reset view returns to the fit, and orbit and zoom are unchanged.
**Consequence.** The board is roughly twice as large on screen by default and the whole flight still fits (cameraFit.test.ts projects the box through a real camera at three stage aspects). The pad corners are no longer guaranteed visible, which is what the lead chose.

## ADR-19: Real shadows on High only (quality pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** DESIGN.md section 2 chose a flat disc over shadow maps for Chromebooks. On a desktop a shadow map is cheap and is most of what makes the board sit on the pad.
**Decision.** On High the key light casts a 2048 px PCF soft shadow through an orthographic camera bounded to the flight volume (`ART.shadowMap`), the board and the shoes cast, the pad slab receives, and the disc is not rendered. Medium and Low keep the disc, which FlightDriver still scales and fades with height. Under the demand frameloop the shadow map updates on every rendered frame, so nothing special is needed for scrubbing.
**Consequence.** On High, `renderer.info.render.calls` counts the shadow pass as well, so the scene draw-call gate is 40 there (ADR-16). The disc numbers in `ART.shadow` stay for the other tiers.

## ADR-20: Feet from real technique (research pass, 2026-11-04)

**Context.** The first feet model (ADR-17) was hand-tuned. A research pass (`docs/RESEARCH-FEET.md`: coaching sources, a TU Delft ollie video analysis, force-plate studies, other skate games) found it got the directions right but several motions wrong, most visibly a kickflip flick that moved straight out with the toes dipped, which is exactly the "flick down" beginner mistake.
**Decision.** `STANCE` and `MOTION` in `src/sim/feet.ts` now follow the report's continuous formulas, still driven by the inputs: the kickflip front foot sits about 45 degrees just behind the bolts with the heel over the heel-side rail; the ollie slide reaches the nose pocket by 0.16 of the airtime; the flick leaves forward, out and up (direction (0.6, 0.3, -0.74), leaning forward for a varial and out for a 360 flip) over 0.05 to 0.22, 0.10 m per rev/s, lighter when the scoop does part of the work; the scoop kicks the back foot behind the heels 0.10 m per rev/s (capped 0.25 m); the back foot starts on its ball with the heel up, across the tail, never turned by the scoop; the feet ride the board in an ollie and lift clear in proportion to flick and scoop. `FootPose` gained `roll`, so the slide and flick are done on the shoe's outside edge and the back shoe sits on the tail kick; FlightDriver maps it per shoe because the front shoe is a mirrored right shoe.
**Not adopted.** An eased board rotation so the catch happens at 0.6 to 0.8 of the airtime (needs `flight.ts`, the totals would not change; adopted in ADR-36 on 2026-11-05); different bails for flip and shove failures; a thinner vulcanised sole (needs `FOOT` and the shoe asset to change together). Heelflips, frontside shove-its and hardflips stay out: flick and scoop are unsigned.
**Consequence.** `feet.test.ts` asserts the researched shapes (45 degree kickflip foot with the heel over the rail, flick forward-out-up with no toe dip, scoop distances, ollie feet on the board). Its no-teleport bound is 3 cm per percent of flight (was 2.5): the hardest scoop is a 25 cm kick in the first eighth of the airtime.

## ADR-21: Speed slider instead of the slow-motion toggle (2026-11-04)

**Context.** Dylan asked for the slow-motion button to become a slider with a wider range that can be changed while the trick plays. Watching the feet needs slower than 0.25x.
**Decision.** "Speed" is a native range over 14 stops, 0.05x to 2x (`SPEEDS` in `src/store/speed.ts`), in the Playback panel above the timeline. It is never disabled; FlightDriver already multiplies the frame delta by the store's rate every frame, so a change applies on the next frame of a flight in the air. `s` still toggles between normal speed and the last slow speed (default 0.25x); `[` and `]` step one stop and announce it. Speed is a view preference: not logged, not persisted, reset to 1x by Reset.
**Consequence.** `PlaybackRate` is a number. The `.toggle` styles stay in app.css but no component uses them.

## ADR-22: Foot trails as one draw call with a progress attribute (2026-11-04)

**Context.** The student should see which way each shoe moves and when, before and during the trick.
**Decision.** `buildFootPaths` (src/scene/footPathGeometry.ts) samples the feet model at 97 points per foot and builds one geometry: a 6-sided tube per foot plus arrowheads where the foot moves, with a per-vertex colour (front sky blue, back yellow, Okabe-Ito) and a per-vertex `aU` (fraction of the airtime). One shared ShaderMaterial draws the part behind the playhead opaque, the rest at 0.3, and the stretch just behind the foot lightened; FlightDriver writes the `uProgress` uniform each frame (a number, no allocation). Before a pop the paths show the flight the current inputs would make, so they move with the sliders. The geometry is rebuilt only when that flight changes. The "Foot motion" panel (`footSteps` in format.ts) is the text equivalent, from the same model.
**Consequence.** One draw call on every tier (measured: Low 14, Medium 16, High 30). The tube is a few thousand triangles. Toggle "Show foot paths" in the View panel, persisted under `trick-lab.foot-paths`.

## ADR-23: The home camera is the rider's own view (2026-11-04)

**Context.** The first view was a 3/4 view from the toe side and in front of the nose, `[1.3, 1.5, 1.9]`, about 27 degrees above the horizon. Dylan orbited by hand to the view a rider has standing on the board, looking down at the deck and their feet, and asked for that to be the first view. The rider stands with the heels on +Z (ADR-24), so the eyes are above the +Z side.

## ADR-24: Goofy stance so the front foot is on the rider's right in the rider's view (2026-11-04)

**Context.** The scene opens on the rider's own view: the camera above the heel side of the board looking down and across it. The user looked at that view and asked for the shoe on the left of the screen to be the back foot (on the tail) and the shoe on the right to be the front foot, the one that slides up and flicks in a kickflip. With the nose kept at +X and the camera on the heel side, that puts the nose at the rider's right: a goofy stance. The regular rider of ADR-10 and ADR-20 had the front foot on the left of that view.
**Decision.** Mirror the rider across the plane z = 0. The board keeps nose +X; the toe side becomes -Z, the heel side +Z, and the rider faces -Z. In `src/sim/feet.ts` every z number (`STANCE` front and back z terms, `MOTION` flick, varial, 360 flip and scoop directions, the bail z) and every yaw (`yawAcross` +87, `yawFlick` -44, `yawScoop` +6, back yaw +95) is negated; the back foot's toe-side clamp becomes `Math.max(-zMax, ...)`. Yaw +90 now points the toe at -Z (three's Y rotation takes +X to (cos, 0, -sin)). The front foot is the right shoe as built and the back foot the mirrored left one (`Feet.tsx`), so the outside-edge sign in FlightDriver's `applyFoot` is +1 for the front shoe and -1 for the back. `ART.stance` is `rollSign = 1`, `yawSign = 1`: a kickflip rolls the grip toward the heel side (+Z, a positive rotation about +X) and a backside shove swings the tail toward the heels (+Z, counterclockwise from above, a positive rotation about +Y). The research in RESEARCH-FEET.md is written relative to the rider (heel-side corner of the nose, outside edge, behind the heels, backside), so it stays true; only the world signs change. Roll keeps its meaning (positive banks onto the outside edge). Pinned by a mirror test in feet.test.ts and by boardPose.test.ts.
**Consequence.** From the rider's view the right-hand shoe slides toward the nose and leaves off the near (heel-side) corner, the board rolls toe edge up toward the camera, a shove-it swings the tail toward the camera and a bail steps toward it. The trick names and the recognizer are unchanged (a goofy kickflip is still a kickflip). Back to regular in one line: negate the z and yaw numbers in `STANCE` and `MOTION` again, swap the `mirror` props and the two edge signs, and set both `ART.stance` signs to -1. A stance switch in the UI would be those same signs read from the store; not built.

## ADR-25: Stance control, goofy by default (2026-11-04)

**Context.** Dylan rides goofy and wants that as the default (ADR-24), but also a control to show a regular rider and switch back. The home view (ADR-23) is the rider's own view from the heel side, and the feet numbers are tuned and tested in the goofy frame.
**Decision.** A "Stance" radio group in the View panel (DESIGN.md 8.6), `stance: 'goofy' | 'regular'` in the ui slice, persisted under `trick-lab.stance`, announced, never logged, not reset, not exported. Regular is derived from goofy, not re-entered: the board turns 180 degrees about Y on the pad, so the nose is at -X on the rider's left, and the feet mirror along the board's length. In `src/sim/feet.ts` the model still computes goofy and `writeFeetPose(..., stance)` applies a last allocation-free step for regular: x to -x and yaw to 180 - yaw (the toe direction mirrored in X, kept in (-180, 180]); y, z, pitch and roll are unchanged. `ART.stance` is a per-stance table: goofy `{ rollSign: 1, yawSign: 1, baseYaw: 0 }`, regular `{ rollSign: -1, yawSign: -1, baseYaw: 180 }`, and `setBoardRotation` writes yaw = baseYaw + yaw * yawSign, roll = roll * rollSign, order 'YXZ' (order 'YZX' since ADR-32, same signs). Both signs flip because the board's own long axis points at -X after the base yaw: a roll of -r about it is +r about world +X, so the grip still rolls toward the heel side (+Z, the camera) first; a yaw of -y swings the tail, now at +X, toward +Z, so a backside shove still sends the tail toward the heels, now clockwise seen from above. Pitch stays nose-up about the board's own Z. The front foot becomes the mirrored left shoe and the back the right one (`Feet.tsx`), and since a mirrored shoe's outside edge is its -Z side, FlightDriver's edge signs swap (front -1, back +1), so a positive roll still banks onto the outside edge. FlightDriver reads `stance` from the store state inside its frame callback; the foot trails rebuild on a change.
**Why the camera does not move.** A real regular rider standing where the goofy one stood would see the nose on the left from the same heel-side spot, so moving the board, not the camera, is what the rider's view of ADR-23 means. Keeping the camera fixed also leaves the fit (ADR-18), the lights and the shadow framing untouched, and makes the switch read as "the other foot forward" rather than a new view. Mirroring the whole world instead would have flipped the camera to the toe side or mirrored the asymmetric board (nose and tail differ, the graphic would read backward).
**Consequence.** Pinned for both stances in boardPose.test.ts (nose +X or -X at rest, kickflip grip toward +Z first, shove tail toward +Z first and turned half round at landing), feet.test.ts (the mirror, the front foot at the nose at -X with toes at -Z, flick and bail toward +Z) and footPathGeometry.test.ts. Trick names and the recognizer are unchanged. `#scene-description` names the stance and the shove's sense.

## ADR-26: Motion cues on the grip show how each shoe will move (2026-11-04)

**Context.** The owner asked for "indicators and arrows showing how the shoe will move against the board to perform the trick". The foot trails (ADR-22) show the whole route of each shoe through the air, which is the wrong question before the pop: a student setting up a kickflip needs to see, on the deck, where the front foot slides and where it leaves, and where the back foot presses and scoops.
**Decision.** Flat arrows lying on the grip, a child of the Board's outer group so they ride with the deck. The description is pure and comes from the feet model: `motionCues(inputs, stance)` in `src/sim/feet.ts` returns, in the board's own frame, the front shoe's ball through its slide and flick (the model's own deck-relative motion, `writeDeckFeet`, from the stance to the end of the flick or the slide), the back shoe's footprint on the tail pocket, and the scoop as an arc about the board's centre (the shove axis) toward the heel side, just in front of the back shoe so the shoe does not hide it, its length the scoop's sideways distance. So an ollie is a straight slide toward the nose, a kickflip slides and then leaves off the heel-side corner, a varial leans forward and a 360 flip out, all from the same `STANCE` and `MOTION` numbers. Regular (ADR-25) turns the board 180 degrees and mirrors the feet along world X, so in the board's frame the cues mirror across its long axis (z to -z, yaw to -yaw). `motionCueGeometry.ts` drapes the description onto whichever grip is higher at each point (the GLB's kicks and concave, or the primitive's flat deck and straight kicks) plus 3 mm, as one geometry with per-vertex colour (front sky blue, back yellow, the trail colours, so the Foot motion legend matches both). One mesh, one shared unlit material (`mat.motionCues`: not tone mapped, no depth writes, polygon offset toward the camera, double-sided in one pass), rebuilt only when flick, scoop, the flight or the stance change (pop height does not move the feet against the deck).
**Fade rule.** Shown when `motionCuesVisible` is on. They are the plan, so they are at full opacity before the pop and FlightDriver fades the shared material out over the first 12 percent of the airtime (one opacity write per frame, no allocation; at zero the material is hidden, so the mesh costs no draw call). They do not come back at the landing: by then the deck has rolled and turned (a pop shove-it lands rotated half round), so an arrow riding it would point the wrong way for the rider. They return when the timeline is scrubbed back to the start and on Reset.
**Consequence.** One draw call on every tier while shown (measured with feet, trails and cues on: Low 15, Medium 17, High 31; budgets 20, 20 and 40). The cue mesh does not cast a shadow. Tested in feet.test.ts (directions for the ollie, kickflip, pop shove-it, varial flip and 360 flip, in both stances) and motionCueGeometry.test.ts (draped above both grips, colours, the press outline outside the sole, the mirror). DESIGN.md 8.7.

## ADR-27: The pop pivots on the tail (2026-11-04)

**Context.** `writePose` pitched the board nose up 18 degrees about the deck centre in the first 5 percent of the airtime while the centre had barely risen, so from 2 to 10 percent the rear wheels sank up to 6 cm into the pad and the tail passed through it. The feet did not follow the tilt, so the back shoe hung above a tail that had dropped away and the front shoe sank into a deck that had risen.
**Decision.** The board pivots on its contact with the pad. `contactLift(pitch, roll)` in `src/sim/flight.ts` is how far the deck centre must rise so no wheel tread edge (both trucks, both sides, the axle tilted by the roll) and not the tail is below the pad; the contact numbers are copies in `CONTACT` (src/sim may not import src/asset), pinned by flight.test.ts to `src/asset/spec.ts`, the built high-detail deck (the tail contact within 1 mm at the strike) and `ART.board`. `POP_PITCH` is now the tail-strike angle, computed from those numbers: 32.5 degrees, where the tail meets the pad with the rear wheels still on it (18 degrees left the tail 5 cm up; RESEARCH-FEET.md puts a real ollie's board at 45 degrees or more once the front foot slides, so this is the conservative end). Up to `POP_RISE_BY` (5 percent) the deck centre's rise is the contact lift alone: the board rocks up on its rear wheels and the tail strikes the pad at full pitch. From the strike to the peak it rises on the projectile's rising half rescaled to run from the height at the strike to the peak, so it rises the whole way, never passes the peak and meets the unchanged parabola at the top; the fall is the parabola itself. Until `LEVEL_BY` the contact lift is also a floor, so a roll in the pop (a kickflip has rolled 18 degrees by the strike) cannot push a wheel into the pad. `heightAt`, the airtime, the peak and the telemetry are unchanged; `writePose(flight, 0)` is exactly the rest pose and progress 1 is exactly the landing height; Pose is still mutated in place with no allocation. The pitch curve keeps its shape (`pitchAt` tests unchanged). The feet ride the deck: `writeDeckFeet` builds the model's motion against the deck and `rideDeck` rotates it by the pitch about the deck centre and adds the rise, and tilts each sole with the deck (pitch * cos yaw toe up, pitch * sin yaw onto the edge, with the shoe's outside-edge sign), so the back shoe rides the tail down to the pad and the front shoe rides the rising deck. The primitive board's trucks and wheels moved to the realistic board's axle line (x 0.208, z 0.078), so Low pivots on the same wheels instead of dipping 3 cm.
**Consequence.** feet.test.ts measures motion "against the board" in the deck's own frame (slide to the nose pocket, flick forward-out-up, the outside-edge bank); the no-teleport bound is 3.2 cm per percent against the deck (the hardest scoop now shows its downward part, 3.08 cm) and 8 cm in the world, where the front foot is carried up to 7 cm per percent by the deck itself in the snap. boardPose.test.ts checks the varial and 360 flip directions with the pitch left out. Checked in the browser at 2, 5, 10 and 20 percent from the home view and a low side view on High, both stances: the rear wheels stay on the pad at 2 percent and the tail touches it at 5. Supersedes the 18 degree cosmetic pitch in ADR-1 and BRIEF.md.
**Known limitation (audit, 2026-11-04).** The landing is prescribed at the rest height (progress 1 is y 0) whatever rotation is left, so a trick landed inside the tolerances with residual roll puts the low side's wheels below the pad (about 24 mm for a tolerated ollie). Fixing it means lifting the landing by the contact lift, a change to the physics contract (`heightAt`, the airtime, the telemetry), so it is deferred. Resolved for landed tricks by ADR-36 (2026-11-05): the catch presses a landed board onto its whole turn by 0.76, so it lands flat; still true for failed ones, whose end pose is unchanged.

## ADR-29: The feet clear the board's actual volume (2026-11-04)

**Context.** The shoes clipped through the board in many poses (owner's screenshots: a kickflip mid-flip with the deck on its edge passing straight through both shoes at the nose and the tail). `src/sim/feet.ts` put the soles on flat heights (`DECK_TOP` 0.0095, `TAIL_TOP` 0.0185), but the rails are 12 mm above the centreline and the kicks rise on a cubic, so the rails and the tail poked through a flat sole even at rest. In the air the only separation was the hover (at most 0.12 m times sin squared of progress), less than the spinning board's reach (rails 0.10 m from the roll axis, wheels 0.126 m) and already small again by 270 degrees, while the gather pulled the feet down to `DECK_TOP` by 0.75.
**Decision.** A clearance step in the sim, pure and allocation-free: after the feet model writes its motion (goofy frame) and before the regular mirror, each shoe rises straight up (world +Y) by the least amount that puts its whole sole 2 mm above the board in the board's pose at that progress (rest with no flight), and no sole corner below the pad. It only adds height; x, z, yaw, pitch and roll stay the model's, and progress 0 of a flight is still exactly the stance. The board is a union of solids in `src/sim/boardVolume.ts`: the realistic deck plate (outline, kicks and concave of `src/asset/deck.ts`, 11.5 mm measured along Y), the primitive deck of Low and the fallback (flat middle and two straight kicks, core plus skin), and per truck three boxes that hold all the hardware of both boards (hanger, wheels, axle ends; one box per truck would either miss the baseplate and pivot cup or swallow the space around the wheels). src/sim may not import src/asset, so `BOARD` is a pinned copy, like `CONTACT` (ADR-27). The sole is the footprint rectangle `FOOT` (0.285 by 0.105), the primitive shoe's box, which holds the GLB shoe's outline. The lift is the larger of two measures, so a thin rail cannot slip between samples: 19 sole samples (corners, edges, centreline) cast up through the board (the boxes by an exact slab test, the deck plate by a march of at most 6 mm steps from where the line leaves the plate's box, then five bisections), and every board edge (the plate's four rails as polylines about 1 cm apart, closed round the tips, and the 12 edges of each box) dropped onto the sole's plane and maximised exactly per segment. Sole samples alone, as first planned, let a rail at an angle pass between two samples (a 6 mm step measured at the toe corner). The test is "the whole sole above the board", not "each sample outside it", so a shoe can never end up under the board with its upper inside it. A board point just outside the sole still counts with a shoulder penalty, 1.5 per metre for the first 5 mm outside and then `far` per metre: without it a rail sweeping in under a shoe from the side lifts it in one step (25 cm for a hard scoop, measured), with it the lift is a ramp. `far` is 1.5 through a landed trick (one gentle slope) and narrows smoothly to 10 over a bail, because a bailing foot steps down beside the board and a gentle slope held it up to 8 cm off the pad (median 4.4 cm over 1,759 failed inputs). The back shoe's roll is no longer a constant (-15): it is the tail kick's chord slope across the sole at the shoe's x, mapped through the yaw the way `rideDeck` maps a deck tilt (about -11 to -14 degrees). Its toe-down pitch is -4 (was -12) and the pop's extra heel lift -5 (was -10): a rigid sole whose toes reach past the toe-side rail pivots on that rail, so every degree of toes down lifts the ball 2 mm off the tail. `MOTION.hover` is unchanged: its 0.13 m peak (with `DECK_TOP`) is the board's swept radius, so the feet already sit as low as a full flip allows, and lowering it would make them bob over each rail and wheel. `DECK_TOP` and `TAIL_TOP` are now aims below the real contact; the clearance sets the soles on the rails (0.0198 m across the flat) and on the tail.
**Why not adjust the stance offsets.** Fixed offsets fit the rest pose only. The deck that clips is a moving one (edge-on at 90 and 270 degrees, upside down at 180, yawed under the back foot in a shove), so the clearance has to know the board's pose at each progress.
**Consequence.** Cost: about 0.01 ms per call on a desktop (two calls a frame; asserted under 0.1 ms in boardVolume.test.ts). boardVolume.test.ts pins the copy by behaviour: the deck top against `midSurface + DECK.thickness / 2` on a grid (1e-9), every vertex of the built deck (both levels) within the 2 mm margin, every vertex of the built trucks and wheels (both levels) and of the primitive board inside the solids, the primitive numbers against `ART.board`, and `deckTop` against `cueHeight`. src/scene/clearance.test.ts is the acceptance test, with the real transforms (`setBoardRotation`, the `applyFoot` Euler, the mirrored left shoe) and a board built from src/asset and `ART.board` directly: for every preset, an ollie, a failed low 360 flip and the hardest inputs, in both stances, at 201 steps of progress, no point of a 21 by 7 sole grid is inside the board, no point of the board's surfaces is inside a shoe (the whole footprint to the GLB toe cap's height plus the primitive upper box), and no sole goes below the pad; it fails on every case with the clearance removed. feet.test.ts: the stance rests on the board, the clearance only adds height, presets land 2 mm above the rails of the landed board, a bail aims at the pad and stands on the failed board only where it lies under a shoe (the failed boards come down rolled or turned, so most bails do), and the lift is continuous: over every preset and pop 0.15 and 0.5 with flick and scoop 0, 1.5 and 3, at 2000 steps, a foot's height changes at most 8.6 mm per 0.001 of progress for a landed trick (the largest in the pop's first steps, the board's own rise) and 3.6 cm for a failed hard shove spinning past the bailing feet, and ten times the resolution gives the same slope, so there is no step left (re-measured for ADR-36 on 2026-11-05: 8.0 mm landed and 10.8 mm in a bail). The model's own motion is measured before the clearance (`writeFeetIntent`), so the 3.2 cm per percent bound against the deck stands; the world bound of 8 cm per percent holds for landed tricks and is 12 cm in a bail (11 cm measured; since ADR-36, 8 cm for both, 7.2 cm landed and 6.6 cm in a bail). Since ADR-36 the clearance does no work in a landed trick after each foot's catch (it held a kickflip's shoes 5 to 6.6 cm up from 0.60 to 0.90 before); in the air it still lifts them by up to 3.75 cm where the tuck does not clear the rolled board. The foot trails sample `writeFeetPose`, so they follow. On High and Medium the soles can sit a few millimetres above the GLB's tail where the primitive's straight kick is higher, since one clearance serves both boards (as the cues do), and the back sole bridges the concave, so its ball is 1 to 2 cm above the pocket's centreline. Draw calls are unchanged (Low 15 before the pop, Medium 16, High 30 after a landing, with feet, trails and cues on).

## ADR-30: Signed flick and scoop give the mirror tricks (2026-11-04)

**Context.** Flick and scoop were unsigned (0 to 3 rev/s), so every flip was a kickflip and every shove backside: the app could not make a heelflip, a frontside shove-it, a hardflip or an inward heelflip, and RESEARCH-FEET.md (sections 1.4 and 1.6, the "not in app" rows of 5.1, item 13 of section 4) listed them as out of scope.

**Decision.** Both rates are signed, -3 to 3 rev/s, step 0.1, default 0; the sign is the direction, in the goofy frame the sim is written in (ADR-10, ADR-24): flick above 0 rolls about +X with the grip turning toward the heel side (+Z) first, a kickflip, below 0 the other way, a heelflip; scoop above 0 swings the tail toward +Z, behind the heels (backside), below 0 toward -Z, in front of the toes (frontside). The flight model already multiplied the rates, so rollTotal and yawTotal simply became signed; boardPose.ts, the regular-stance turn (ADR-25) and the contact lift are unchanged, and a test (src/scene/mirrorTricks.test.ts) pins that a heelflip and a frontside shove keep their direction for the regular rider.

- Recognition: flips and half shoves are a symmetric round of the signed totals (sign(x) * round(|x|), so -0.5 rounds like +0.5, and -0 is normalised to 0). rollError and yawError stay signed about the nearest turn. "Under-rotated" means the magnitude fell short of the nearest whole turn in the direction of rotation, "over-rotated" past it (`isUnderRotated`, a magnitude test; a rotation that rounds to no turn is over). failedBy keeps its sign; the copy shows the magnitude. isLanded is unchanged and allocation-free.
- Naming table, keyed on signed (flips, half shoves): (0,0) Ollie; (1,0) Kickflip; (-1,0) Heelflip; (2,0) Double kickflip; (-2,0) Double heelflip; (0,1) Pop shove-it; (0,-1) Frontside pop shove-it; (0,2) 360 shove-it; (0,-2) Frontside 360 shove-it; (1,1) Varial flip; (1,-1) Hardflip; (-1,-1) Varial heelflip; (-1,1) Inward heelflip; (1,2) 360 flip; (1,-2) 360 hardflip; (-1,-2) Laser flip; (-1,2) 360 inward heelflip; (2,2) Double tre. Anything else is "Unnamed" with direction words, for example "Unnamed (2 heelflips, 1 frontside half shove)" or "Unnamed (0 flips, 3 backside half shoves)".
- Presets, keys 1 to 8, all at pop 0.35 m, each landing there and failing under-rotated at 0.15 m (tricks.test.ts): 1 Kickflip (flick 1.9, scoop 0); 2 Heelflip (-1.9, 0); 3 Pop shove-it (0, 0.9); 4 Frontside shove-it (0, -0.9); 5 Varial flip (1.9, 0.9); 6 Varial heelflip (-1.9, -0.9); 7 Hardflip (1.9, -0.9); 8 360 flip (1.9, 1.9). Objectives follow the presets ("Land a heelflip", "Land a frontside shove-it", ...), eight in all.
- Feet: the magnitude drives every amount, the sign the direction. Heelflip: front foot toward x 0.11, z -0.02, yaw 88 per unit of heelflip factor (the ball more centred, the toes over the toe-side rail), back foot toward x -0.32, yaw 100; slide 8 cm (`distanceHeel` -0.08); the kick is the kickflip's flick with z negated, (0.60, 0.30, -0.74), off the toe-side corner of the nose, heel last, toes up 20 degrees at the peak instead of the outside-edge roll. Frontside: back foot further back and toward the heel side of the tail (x -0.04, z +0.02, yaw -10 per unit, near -0.33, +0.04, 90 at the preset), front foot's z -0.04 per unit; the scoop is the backside scoop with z negated, (0, -0.3, -0.95), the same 10 cm per rev/s. The research numbers are RESEARCH-FEET.md's, written with the toe side at +Z, so z and yaw are negated here. The bail still steps off to the heel side. Positive inputs give the pre-change poses to the last bit (src/sim/feet.pinned.test.ts); the clearance (ADR-29) needed no change and its acceptance test now also runs (-3, -3), (-3, 3) and (3, -3).
- Copy: the slider reads the magnitude with the direction word ("1.9 rev/s heelflip", "0.0 rev/s" with no word; aria-valuetext "1.9 revolutions per second, heelflip"); totals read the same way everywhere (card "365 deg heelflip, 173 deg frontside shove", announcer "365 degrees of heelflip, 173 degrees of frontside shove", the strip "365°" over "heelflip" in the new `.telemetry__dir`). The stance note names both directions for both axes.

**Why not a direction toggle beside each slider.** A toggle doubles the controls and the tab stops and hides the fact that a heelflip is the same physics as a kickflip turning the other way. A slider centred on 0 shows it: left of centre is one trick, right of centre its mirror, and dragging across 0 passes through the ollie.

**Consequence.** Kickflip copy changed on purpose: "365 degrees of flip" is now "365 degrees of kickflip" and "365 deg flip" is "365 deg kickflip", so every non-zero total names its direction; the store and UI tests changed with it. ## ADR-31: A textured ground, fog and an outdoor environment instead of a slab in a void (2026-11-04)

**Context.** The board sat on a 1.6 x 1.2 m flat grey box with black edge lines and a centre mark, in a void of the theme's clear colour, lit by a key, an ambient and three's indoor RoomEnvironment. Next to the realistic board and shoes the pad read as a placeholder, and the edge of the slab was the most prominent line in every low orbit.

**Decision.** Four parts, no new dependency, no post-processing, no extra render pass.
- A concrete ground built by the asset pipeline (`src/asset/groundSpec.ts`, `groundTextures.ts`, `ground.ts`, built through `scripts/lib/build.ts` into `public/models/ground.glb`): one texture tile is one 1.6 x 1.2 m slab bay (the old pad) with a 12 mm wide, 4 mm deep expansion joint along its edges and a faint worn painted centre mark where the mark mesh was, baked into a 1024 px grey colour map (tinted by the base colour factor, like the medium grip), a 1024 px normal map and a 256 px roughness map; seeded, deterministic, tileable. One 40.0 x 39.6 m quad (25 x 33 bays, odd so a bay centre is under the board) whose UVs repeat the tile by `KHR_texture_transform`. Its own budget: 500 KB, 1 draw call, 64 triangles, 3 textures at 1024 px (measured 434.9 KB, 1, 2, 3); `assets:check` also checks the size (at least 24 m, odd whole bays), the surface on y 0 and the centring. Medium and High load it lazily like the board; Low and the fallback draw an untextured plane of the same size and the concrete's mean colour (one draw call), so Chromebooks download and pay nothing extra. The slab, edge lines and mark mesh are gone on every tier: two draw calls fewer.
- Linear fog to the clear colour (near 5.5 m, far 15 m), so the ground fades into the background. The fog colour is the clear colour per theme; nothing else about the scene depends on the theme. Near and far are tested against the farthest board point and the nearest visible plane edge. (By distance from the camera since ADR-55, 2026-11-09: by view depth, a stage wider than about 3.6:1 saw the plane's edge.)
- A procedural outdoor environment (`src/scene/outdoorEnvironment.ts`: sky dome with a vertical gradient, a sun disc in the key light's direction, concrete below) prefiltered once through PMREMGenerator exactly as before, and a hemisphere light (sky above, concrete below) instead of the ambient light. **Consequence.** Draw calls with feet, trails and cues on, before a pop: Low 13, Medium 15, High 29 (were 15, 17, 31). Every bay repeats the same tile, mark included; fog hides the repetition in low orbits, and a high orbit shows it.

## ADR-32: The board pitches before it rolls: Euler order YZX (2026-11-04)

**Context.** ADR-2 chose 'YXZ' (yaw, then roll about the board's long axis, then the nose-up pitch) when the pitch was a cosmetic few degrees. Since ADR-27 the pop pitches the board 32.5 degrees nose up and the flip starts at once, and with the pitch applied last, about the transverse axis of the already rolled board, the raised nose swung sideways by x sin(pitch) sin(roll): 11 cm toward the heel side at 14 percent of a kickflip (measured in the running scene), the other way for a heelflip, back to the axis as the pitch decayed by 35 percent. The feet, which do not follow the board, then sat over the wrong part of the deck (a varial flip's front toe appeared over the toe-side rail, a hardflip's nose left the flicking foot behind). boardPose.test.ts and mirrorTricks.test.ts had worked around the symptom by zeroing the pitch.

**Decision.** `setBoardRotation` writes order 'YZX': yaw about world Y (the shove, with the stance's base yaw), then the pop pitch about the yawed transverse axis, then the roll about the pitched long axis. The flip axis still follows the shove (ADR-2's reason for yaw first) and the nose stays on it while the board flips. `boardMatrix` in boardVolume.ts (ADR-29) builds the same composition, so the clearance solver sees the board the scene draws; `contactLift` in flight.ts computes the pop's lowest point for the same composition: a point (x, y, z) ends at height x sin p + (y cos r - z sin r) cos p and a tread edge's lowest point is R sqrt(1 - cos^2 p sin^2 r) below its centre (both reduce to the old numbers at roll 0, so the tail-strike pin holds). Tests: the nose stays in the board's vertical plane through the pop of a kickflip and a heelflip in both stances; the scene's Euler and the solver's matrix agree on a mixed pose; the varial, 360 flip, heelflip and frontside direction tests now use the full pose, pitch included.

**Consequence.** Nothing changes at rest, at landing or in a shove-only trick. In the first third of every flip the nose no longer drifts sideways. The strike-time contact lift of a rolled board moved by under a millimetre, and that is blended into the thrown height until 50 percent (boardHeightAt), so the pinned feet rows (feet.pinned.test.ts) moved by up to 0.5 mm through rideDeck and were re-recorded. Reviewed before the change by a gpt-6-astra agent at medium effort (the formula, the test list and the "nothing changes after 35 percent" claim were corrected from that review).

## ADR-36: The board spins up, spins free and is caught (2026-11-05)

**Context.** `writePose` turned the board at a constant rate from progress 0 to 1 (`roll = rollTotal * u`, ADR-1), so there was no moment at which the feet could catch it. A model audit (`docs/research/2026-11-05/model-audit.md`, opus) measured what that did to the kickflip preset: the board had rolled 18 degrees at the tail strike and 58 by 0.16 while the front foot was still dragging up the grip, it was edge-on at 0.70 and still 31 degrees short at 0.90, the feet gathered over the bolts on a fixed clock (0.35 to 0.75) above an upside-down board, and the clearance (ADR-29) held both shoes 5.0 to 6.6 cm up on the spinning board from 0.60 to 0.90, so they met the grip only as the wheels touched. **Decision.** `src/sim/flight.ts` exports `PHASE` (pop end 0.05; flipUp 0.06 to 0.16; shoveUp 0 to 0.10; catch 0.64 to 0.76), the one source of truth that the feet and the copy read. Per axis the rate is `W * ramp(u) * (1 - decay(u))`, a smoothstep ramp over the up window and one minus a smoothstep over the catch, with `1 / W = mid(catch) - mid(up)` (W 1.69 for the flip, 1.54 for the shove) so that it integrates to 1; `spinFraction` is the closed-form integral R(u), three pieces, no loop. The free rotation is `total * R(u)`, which reaches the total at `catch.end` and holds it. A landed flight also subtracts its residual error over the catch with a smoothstep, so it ends on the exact whole turn and half turn; a failed flight is not corrected and ends at its totals, as before. Landing is decided by `isLanded`, moved with the tolerances and `turns` into `src/sim/landing.ts` (tricks.ts re-exports them) so that flight.ts does not import tricks.ts. `rollAt` and `yawAt` serve `writePose` and `boardHeightAt` (the contact lift now uses the roll at p). `Flight` gains `landed`, `rollTarget`, `yawTarget`, `flipRate` and `shoveRate` (the free-phase rates, `W * |flick|` and `W * |scoop|`, magnitudes). The slider keeps its meaning (option 1 of the audit's section 3.6): `rollTotal = flick * T * 360` and `yawTotal = scoop * T * 360`, so the presets, the tolerances and recognition are unchanged, and the free rate is shown as telemetry ("Air spin", `spinReadout` in format.ts). The kickflip preset now reads 0 degrees at the strike, 31 at 0.16, 68 at 0.22, 242 at 0.5, 328 at 0.64 and 360 from 0.76, and spins at 3.2 rev/s in the air against the slider's 1.9.
The feet (`src/sim/feet.ts`) are timed against the catch. `CATCH.front` is 0.73 and `CATCH.back` 0.76. The gather runs from 0.44 (`catch.start - 0.2`) to each foot's catch. The shared `hover * sin^2(pi u)` became a per-foot tuck (`tuckShape`): 0 at the foot's release (front at the end of the flick, back at the end of the pop or of the scoop), an ease-out rise to its peak (0.40 front, 0.35 back), an ease-in descent to 0 at its catch, amplitude `hoverHeight`. A landed foot aims at `LAND_Y`, the height at which the clearance rests a sole on the rails over the bolts (about 0.021, was `DECK_TOP` 0.0095). From the catch's start to each foot's catch the board's residual roll and yaw are carried onto the foot with a growing weight (`rideCatch`), so the sole sits flat on the settling deck. The bail starts at `catch.start` (was 0.62). The flick and the slide (ADR-33 to ADR-35) and the clearance itself are unchanged. `footSteps` ends the lift at the catch, adds "Both feet catch the board at about 70 percent of the flight and press it flat." for a landed flight whose feet left the board, and ends with "Both feet ride the board down over the bolts and land on the grip."; `describeScene` says "Feet on the board, riding it down." after the catch. FlightDriver needed no change.
**Supersedes.** ADR-1 in part (the totals stand, the constant rate does not); the ADR-17 consequence (the fixed hover, the gather over the bolts); ADR-20's "Not adopted" eased rotation, now adopted; ADR-27's known limitation, resolved for landed tricks (a landed board is flat from 0.76, so no wheel ends below the pad) and still true for failed ones; ADR-29's consequence numbers, updated there. The rotation line in `docs/BRIEF.md`, the flight phases in `docs/BRIEF-FEET.md` and item 11 of RESEARCH-FEET.md section 4 (closed) follow.
**Tests changed on purpose.** flight.test.ts: "rotates at a constant rate" is replaced by the profile tests (R(0) = 0, R(catch.end) = 1, R monotonic, a continuous rate that is 0 before the ramp and after the catch, the free rate equal to `flipRate` and `shoveRate` within 1e-9, landed presets on exact whole turns, failed flights at their totals, the kickflip under 1 degree at the strike and under 35 at the end of the ramp, the rest pose at 0, allocation-free), and the pivot's lead over the parabola at 0.02 is pinned to the pivot lift with no roll (2.3 cm at pop 0.6, over 3 while the constant-rate roll added its own lift). feet.pinned.test.ts is re-recorded from u = 0.04 (at 0.04 to 0.2 only the heights move). feet.test.ts: `onDeck` takes out each foot's tuck; the no-teleport and continuity bounds are re-measured (the bail's world bound tightened from 12 to 8 cm); new tests pin the catch times, the tuck's shape, the gather to the catch and the feet on the board after the catch. boardPose.test.ts and mirrorTricks.test.ts read their quarter, half and tenth turns where R(u) puts them instead of at u = 0.25, 0.5 and 0.1, with the same thresholds. format.test.ts: the kickflip and heelflip scripts gain the catch and the ride-down, plus the step timing and the spin readout. ui.test.tsx: the telemetry strip has a fifth value. tricks.test.ts and clearance.test.ts are unchanged and green.
**Consequence.** Measured on the eight presets and on the same tricks rescaled to land at pop 0.2 and 0.5 (24 flights): from each foot's catch to the landing the clearance lift is 0 and every sole is 2 mm (the margin) above the deck, where the kickflip's shoes used to be held 5 to 6.6 cm up. ## ADR-38: Fitted PCF shadows on High, projected footprints on Medium and Low, a detail camera preset (2026-11-05)

**Context.** Items 3, 4 and 7 of section 8 of `docs/research/2026-11-05/visual-realism.md`. (a) High asked for `PCFSoftShadowMap`, deprecated since three r182; r187 warned and substituted PCF at radius 1. Its orthographic shadow camera was a fixed +-1.0 m box, near 1 and far 12, and the normal bias 0.015 m was thicker than the 11.5 mm deck. (b) Medium and Low drew one board-sized ellipse fixed at x z 0 that shrank to 0.8 with height, so it did not fall where High's shadow falls, and the shoes had no shadow at all. (c) The only fixed view was the rider's view from 60 degrees up (ADR-23), which hides the deck's thickness, the trucks and the gap between sole and grip.
**Decision.**
1. *High.* `PCFShadowMap` with `shadow.radius` 3. The shadow camera is fitted once (`shadowFrustum` in cameraFit.ts) to two caster boxes in `ART.shadowMap.casters` and to where those boxes' shadows reach the pad, as the key light sees them, plus 3 cm. ## ADR-39: The shoe is built like a shoe, and Medium gets its silhouette (2026-11-05)

**Context.** `docs/research/2026-11-05/visual-realism.md` section 3 and items 1 and 2 of section 8 found the shoe the weakest footwear cue in the scene: on High a smoothly lofted upper whose ankle "opening" was a sunk valley (it read as a sculpted groove), painted eyelets, 4.4 mm round tube laces, one slab of sole with a painted stripe and a 16 x 64 band texture (no tread, so the sole is blank whenever a flick turns it to the camera), and a pristine weave; on Medium and Low a sole slab and an upper box. High (`shoe.ts`): the shell is cut open over the ankle and the lace gap and finished with a tube round the rim (a 4.5 mm padded collar easing to a 1.3 mm edge bead), closed inside by a lining and an insole; the tongue is a separate padded slab 3.5 mm under the eyestays and 3 mm wider than the gap each side, rising out of it in front of the ankle; twelve raised eyelet rims; flat ribbon laces with a bar, ten crossings (one 2 mm over the other), a knot and two ends with aglets; a 4 mm toe spring in the upper and the foxing only (the underside stays flat on y 0, and the toe cap height at t 0.8 that the clearance test assumes does not move); a groove between the foxing and the outsole edge; a toe bumper in the sole material. The footprint outline goes from 48 to 72 points per side; the upper loft goes from 73 x 48 to 69 stations of 2 x 22 points (60 cosine spaced plus extra ones at the ends of the opening). The upper material is double sided, so the inside of the shell (dark suede) shows wherever the opening lets a view in, instead of the ground through a culled face. Textures (`shoeTextures.ts`, seed 20261105): the upper map is 1024 x 1024 but one grey channel tinted by the base colour factor, the medium grip's trick (the same picture as RGB measured 420.4 KB against 254.2, which would have taken the file to about 686 KB, over its 600 KB gate); the sole map is 1024 x 512 RGB, an atlas of the side wall by arc position round the footprint (so wear can sit on one side), the bumper and the waffle tread in plan. Mild wear: the lateral forefoot patch with its dark core, a scuffed toe, four vamp creases, foxing and bumper scuffs at the same places, a non-periodic two-octave nap. Medium and Low (`resources.ts`, numbers in `ART.feet`): the GLB's footprint, centred across, as an extruded sole with a bottom bevel and a foxing step, and a 22 x 14 arch loft with a heel counter, a collar notch and a valley, still two geometries in the two primitive materials.

## ADR-41: The hardflip is removed (2026-11-05)

**Context.** The demo is public, and the owner judged the hardflip inaccurate: fix it or remove it, and remove it if a fix would mean guessing. Research on 2026-11-05 (`docs/research/2026-11-05/hardflip.md`: 17 coaching sources, the physics and high-speed footage literature, a numerical check with the board's inertia) found the outline agreed (frontside scoop, the board between the legs, the front foot catching first) and every number missing or contested. Skaters recognise two styles with different board paths (Skateology 2011-01-05, Braille, skatedeluxe). The one published formula for the vertical style (Carlisle et al., arXiv:2108.06307, 2021-08-13) is kinematic and called "not physical" by its authors. Nothing measures the path, its timing, or the feet. ADR-35's forward flick, written from memory (RESEARCH-FEET.md item 16), is contradicted by 9 of the 12 sources that state a direction.

**Decision.**
- The Hardflip preset and its objective "Land a hardflip" are removed: seven presets and seven objectives. The 360 flip moves from key 8 to key 7; key 8 does nothing.
- The naming table drops (1,-1) Hardflip and (1,-2) 360 hardflip. A kickflip with a frontside scoop still lands or fails by the same rule and reads "Unnamed (1 kickflip, 1 frontside half shove)" (or "2 frontside half shoves"). The ids `hardflip` and `360-hardflip` are gone from `TrickId` and `PresetId`.
- **Motion unchanged.** `feet.ts` keeps ADR-35's lean and toe turn for that combination (only its comments change), so nothing on the sliders moves. Removing them would hand the combination the varial flip's sideways lean, which ADR-35 judged wrong, and no source-backed replacement exists. Checked on 2026-11-05 by fingerprinting `src/sim` on main and on this change over 1,530 input combinations (pop 0.15, 0.35 and 0.6; 15 flicks; 17 scoops; both stances; 41 samples a flight): board pose, feet, legs, motion cues, foot steps and feet text are byte-identical, and the only differences are the trick name in the result, card and scene description for exactly the two removed names.
- Tests: the sweeps that looped over the presets (feet on the bolts, clearance, rest stance, tuck and catch, legs, step timing) now add the kickflip with a frontside scoop explicitly, so their coverage is what it was. ADR-35's behaviour tests stay, on the inputs instead of the preset. The naming, preset, objective, store and UI tests change on purpose.

**Not adopted.** Fixing the hardflip: it would mean choosing a style, a board path and every foot number without evidence. Changing the other tricks: their foot setups come from sections of RESEARCH-FEET.md that were read, and nothing found on 2026-11-05 shows any of them to be wrong; improving them needs the reference capture (CAPTURE-PROTOCOL.md), not more reading.

**Consequence.** Supersedes ADR-35 as a hardflip; its motion stays, unnamed and unverified. The presets grid (two columns) now ends with the 360 flip alone on its last row, and both side columns are shorter, so the column fit noted in ADR-30 can only improve. To bring the hardflip back: capture both styles, pick one on purpose, fit the board as a rotation path rather than two independent Euler angles, and fit the feet from the same trials (`docs/research/2026-11-05/hardflip.md` section 3).

## ADR-43: A game of SKATE against the computer (2026-11-06)

**Context.** The owner found the lab dull and asked for it to play more like a game, then picked the classic skate park game of SKATE from a list of ideas. ADR-42 is taken by the hardflip estimate on its own unmerged branch (`t3/hardflip-estimate`), so this is 43.

**Decision.**
- **Rules** (`src/sim/skate.ts`, pure). The computer sets first. When the setter lands a trick, the other player has to copy it: the same trick, the same way round (a heelflip is not a kickflip). A missed copy is a letter; the fifth letter (S K A T E) loses. The setter keeps setting until a set misses, then the turn passes. A student's set must be a named trick with a flip or a shove: an ollie or an unnamed trick keeps the turn and says why.
- **The computer plays by the same physics.** It does not roll for land or bail. It aims at the inputs that land its trick dead on (`aimFor`: pop 0.35 m, raised on the slider grid only when a rate would pass 2.6 rev/s), misses the flick and the scoop by a normal draw with standard deviation `spread` x (0.25 + rate), clamped to 3 deviations, and the real flight and `recognize` decide the result. So big rates and two-axis tricks are harder for it, as for a skater. Levels: Easy (spread 0.10, calls the 4 single flips and shove-its), Normal (0.06, the 7 presets), Hard (0.035, all 15 named tricks but the ollie). Measured over 4,000 tries each (seed 42): Normal lands a kickflip 78 percent of the time, a pop shove-it 93, a varial flip 72, a 360 flip 53, a double tre 31; Easy a kickflip 55; Hard a kickflip 96.
- **Store.** A fifth slice, `skate` (`src/store/skate.ts`), owns the game, its last message and the session's wins. `createTrickStore(now, random)` takes the random source so tests script the computer. `popTrick` asks `computerGo()` who pops: on the computer's turn it pops the computer's inputs and leaves the sliders alone (the scene already follows the flight's own inputs). `resolve` hands every result to `scoreSkate`. `flightBy` says whose flight is on screen; the computer's tricks never complete an objective, and are announced and carded as the computer's.
- **No do-overs.** During a game a paused, unlanded trick must be resumed before the next pop, presets are off (aria-disabled, with a note; keys 1 to 7 announce why), and Reset ends the game.
- **Log.** New events: `skate.started` (level), `skate.set` (by, trick), `skate.letter` (player, letters so far), `skate.ended` (winner or null, reason spelled, quit, restart or reset, letter counts). `trick.popped` and `trick.resolved` gain `by`; the computer's `trick.popped` also carries its `call`. The level is not logged on its own: `skate.started` carries it.
- **UI.** A "Game of SKATE" panel tops the results column (DESIGN.md 11): the rules, a Computer level radio group and Start game between games; both players' letters, what just happened and what to do next during one. The Pop button reads "Computer's go" on the computer's turn, so Space drives the whole game. Starting and quitting move focus to the panel heading (not a button, so Space pops from there).

**Not adopted.** A coin flip for the computer's land or bail: it would teach nothing and could contradict the board on screen. Hiding the slider numbers and a timing meter: good next steps, left out to keep this change to one mode. Points or a stored high score: a game of SKATE is scored in letters; the session's wins show in the panel title.

**Consequence.** The README's "not a skateboarding game" now says the game is a small one on top of the lab. The results column is one panel taller, so at 1280 x 800 it scrolls a little more (it already scrolled since ADR-30). Tests: `src/sim/skate.test.ts` (the aim lands every named trick, seeded landing rates by level and trick, every rule transition), `src/store/skate.test.ts` (whole games both ways, logging, presets, pause, quit and reset), `src/ui/Skate.test.tsx` (keyboard only: level, start, Space for the computer's go, copy, quit, focus).

## ADR-44: The game of SKATE follows the real game's rules (2026-11-08)

**Context.** The owner asked for the game to play the way a real game of SKATE is played, and picked four rules from a list: a coin flip for who starts, calling your trick before you set, two tries on the last letter, and no repeated tricks. ADR-43 had the computer always set first, let the student set any landed trick without calling it, and only kept the computer from calling its own last trick.

**Decision.**
- **Coin flip.** `startSkate` draws once from the store's random source (`coinFlip`: below one half is the student). `skate.started` logs `first`. The winner sets first. A game does not start while a trick is in the air, playing or paused, so a flight from before it can never score against its first call.
- **Call your trick.** On their set turn the student calls a trick (`callSkateTrick`, a native select grouped Flips, Shove-its, Flip and shove) before they can pop; a pop without a call is refused and says why. The set counts only if they land exactly that trick; a miss or a different trick (named, unnamed or an ollie) passes the set to the computer with no letter, as in the real game. The call cannot change while a trick is in the air. It is not logged on its own: in a game the student's `trick.popped` carries `call`. The computer already called its tricks; it now picks its call as soon as its set turn begins (`withComputerCall`), so the student sees what it is going for before it pops.
- **Two tries on the last letter.** A copier with S K A T who misses gets a second try at the same trick (`secondTry`), announced; the second miss is the E. It applies to both players.
- **No repeats.** A trick set and landed by the setter is used up for the rest of the game (`used`), for both players. The computer moves on to the next level's tricks when its own run out (Easy has four), and in the very long game where a set uses up the last of the 15, the slate is wiped and a fresh one starts with that trick (`markUsed`), so no-repeats keeps holding. It still avoids its own last call while it has a choice. A trick the setter missed is not used up.
- **Trick guide.** `trickGuide` says in words which way and how far to flick and scoop ("Toward kickflip (slider to the right), for one full flip."), plus one tip on trading pop against spin. It never shows a slider value: the owner chose a guide without numbers so copying stays a skill.
- **Ollie.** Still not settable (anyone copies it, so it only wastes a turn); it is left out of the picker.

**Not adopted.** Letting the student set without calling (the old rule): in the real game the setter says the trick first. A "prove it" rule (the setter re-lands after a last-letter copy): a house rule, not common enough. Showing the computer's slider values after it lands: the owner chose the guide instead.

**Consequence.** The random source now also drives the coin flip, so scripted tests queue their draws (`src/store/skate.test.ts`). `SkateGame` gains `first`, `used`, `lastCall` (was `lastSet`) and `secondTry`. The panel gains the call picker and the guide (DESIGN.md 11); the screen-by-screen redesign follows in a later change.

## ADR-45: A welcome screen and three screens (2026-11-08)

**Context.** The owner found the app cluttered and overwhelming: the lab and the game of SKATE shared one page, so a first visit faced three columns of controls at once. They asked for a landing page with a preview, a title, a description and two choices, play a game of SKATE or practice in the lab, and picked a live preview of the board doing tricks (a still on low-power devices and under reduced motion).

**Decision.**
- **Screens.** `screen` in the ui slice: `home`, `skate` or `lab`. `setScreen` changes it, asks for focus on the new screen's one h1 (`#screen-heading`, `{ kind: 'screen-heading' }`), and is not logged (it is navigation, like the UI preferences). Leaving the game ends it: a game in play is logged as `skate.ended` with reason quit, before anything else, so a trick in the air can no longer score in it. A scrub gesture still open is committed (its timeline is about to unmount), then a trick in the air (playing or paused) lands at once, as under reduced motion (ADR-6), so no screen inherits a flight it has no controls to finish.
- **Landmarks.** Each screen has one `main`: the welcome text, the lab's Setup column, the game's column. The stage stays a named region and the lab's results an `aside`.
- **URL.** The screen is mirrored in the hash (`#/skate`, `#/lab`, none for home). The store reads it at creation; `App` pushes a history entry on each change and follows `popstate` and `hashchange`, so back, forward, bookmarks and the GitHub Pages base path all work with no router dependency. A hash that already means the screen showing (an unknown one means home) is tidied with `replaceState`, so it never adds a Back step.
- **Layout.** One grid, picked by `data-screen` on `.app`: home is the welcome text beside the stage; the lab keeps its three columns under a top bar; the game is the stage beside one game column under the top bar. App renders every child in a fixed slot (false holds the place), so the stage and the 3D view are never remounted by a screen change. The top bar holds Home, the screen's h1, Shortcuts, the Light theme switch and a button across to the other mode; the welcome screen has Keyboard shortcuts and the same switch under its choices. Both moved out of the lab's View panel, so every screen has a DOM control for each key it answers to.
- **Preview.** On the home screen FlightDriver poses the board, feet and legs from the preview's own clock (`src/scene/showcase.ts`): six presets on a loop at half speed, each holding on the pad, flying, then holding at its landing. It never touches the store's flight, so it logs and scores nothing, and FlightDriver stays the only pose writer. The camera holds a `showcase` preset (a three-quarter view, 28 degrees up, 35 degrees round) with the orbit off; motion cues and foot trails are hidden (they are a plan for the student's inputs). `previewCanPlay` (store) is true only on home with motion allowed, a 3D view and a tier above Low; there a "Pause preview" button sits on the stage (WCAG 2.2.2: motion that starts by itself and lasts more than five seconds must be pausable), and the choice is persisted under `trick-lab.preview`, announced and not logged. `previewAnimates` is `previewCanPlay` and not paused; only then is the frameloop "always". Otherwise one still frame shows the kickflip caught at 45 percent. Numbers in `ART.showcase`.
- **Keys per screen.** `SCREEN_KEYS` in App: theme and `?` everywhere; the game adds Space and the speed keys but not the preset keys or `r` (Reset would end the game); the lab has all of them and `p` (the perf badge's switch is in its View panel). The shortcuts dialog lists only the current screen's keys.
- **Text equivalent.** On home the stage region is named "Preview" and `#scene-description` reads a sentence about the preview instead of the board state.

**Not adopted.** A router library: three screens and a hash need none. A confirmation before leaving a game in play: the game screen (next change) decides how to guard it. A pre-recorded video for the preview: it would go stale whenever the look changes, and the live scene costs nothing extra once loaded.

**Consequence.** Measured on the welcome screen with the preview playing (dev build, 1440 x 900): High 30 draw calls, Medium 15, Low 13 and on demand (the still). At 960 px and below the stage height is now `clamp(220px, 62.5vw, 560px)` instead of a 16:10 aspect ratio with a 260 px floor, which pushed the page 26 px wider than a 390 px phone. Tests: `src/ui/Screens.test.tsx` (keyboard into each screen, focus, URL, back button, keys per screen, leaving a game), `src/store/screens.test.ts`, `src/scene/showcase.test.ts`; the lab's and the game's UI tests now start on their screen.

## ADR-46: The game screen (2026-11-08)

**Context.** With the game on its own screen (ADR-45), the owner asked that it be clear whose turn it is, computer or student, and what the trick is, in a seamless, friendly experience, and chose: only the essentials on screen (the turn, the trick, both players' letters, the board, the three sliders and Pop), the computer taking its go on its own after a short pause with a way to stop that, and a trick guide without numbers (ADR-44).

**Decision.**
- **One column, only the game.** `main.app__play` holds `Skate` alone: between games, How to play (the six rules), the computer's level and Start; in a game, the letters, the turn card, the sliders with Pop and two switches, and the last trick's outcome; at the end, who won with Play again and Change level. The presets, telemetry grid, foot steps, objectives, event log, speed slider, timeline, Pause and Reset are not on this screen. DESIGN.md 11.
- **Whose turn, and the trick.** `turnView` (pure, `src/sim/skate.ts`) gives the card's words: "Your turn" or "Computer's turn", what they are doing ("Copy the computer's", "Your set", "Going for", "Copying your") and the trick. The card is tinted for the player up and the trick is the largest text in the column; the letters mark the player up; a chip over the stage's corner repeats the turn and the trick (decorative, `aria-hidden`). Words always carry it, so colour is never the only cue.
- **The computer goes on its own.** `useComputerTurns` in `Skate.tsx`: on the computer's turn, with nothing in the air, auto on and no dialog open, it calls `popTrick` after `COMPUTER_DELAY_MS` (2200 ms, long enough to read the last result and see the computer's call; a countdown bar fills over `--skate-turn-delay`, tested equal). The same action as the button and Space, so the store decides everything and nothing new is logged. One rule (`computerWaiting`) decides both the timer and the bar, and the bar shows only while the wait runs, so after a dialog both start over together; the callback checks the rule once more before it pops. "Computer goes on its own" (`skateAuto`, persisted under `trick-lab.skate-auto`, default on, announced, not logged) switches it off; then the computer waits for "Computer's go" or Space, as in ADR-43. Reduced motion and a failed 3D view do not hold it: they are about motion, and under both the computer's trick resolves at once with no animation; the pace is the switch's job, which every student has.
- **A clean start.** `startSkate` puts the board back on the pad and clears the last result (the speed is kept), and the coin flip is the turn card's first news.
- **Focus.** Starting a game moves focus to the turn heading (Space pops from there); turns never move it (the Pop button is the same element for both players, relabelled). The end of a game moves focus to the result heading, because the turn controls under it are gone: the one exception to "resolving a trick never moves focus", made so focus is never dropped to the page. Quitting moves it to How to play.
- **Leaving a game in play asks first.** `requestLeave` (the top bar's Home and other mode, and Quit game) opens a native modal dialog, "Leave this game?" or "Quit this game?", with Keep playing first so the modal focuses the safe choice; Leave or Quit ends the game (logged as quit, ADR-45). Esc keeps playing. The browser's Back still leaves at once (its navigation cannot be held), and any leave still waiting is dropped. A game that ends behind the dialog (a trick already in the air lands) drops the leave too: the dialog closes and focus goes to the result. A modal open holds the keyboard shortcuts and the computer's turn.
- **Keys.** The game answers to Space, `s` (its Slow motion switch), `t` and `?`, and Space and `s` only while a game is in play, when Pop and Slow motion are on screen; the speed steps `[` and `]` and the timeline's Page keys are the lab's only, since the game has no speed slider or timeline.
- **Phones.** Below 960 px the game's stage is sticky at the top of the page, so the trick stays in view while the student scrolls down to the sliders and Pop. Its height, `--stage-sticky-h`, is `clamp(150px, min(50vw, 40vh), 420px)`: half the width, but never more than 40 percent of the height, so a phone held sideways (844 x 390: 156 px) keeps the controls usable. `scroll-padding-top` on the page and `scroll-margin-top` on the column's elements leave that height clear, so a focused control never sits under the stage (WCAG 2.4.11).
- **Storage helpers.** `readPref` and `savePref` move to `src/store/prefs.ts` so the skate slice can persist its switch the same way the ui slice does.

**Not adopted.** A countdown in seconds (the bar and "Goes in a moment." say enough without making the student watch numbers). Showing the telemetry grid in the game: the owner chose the essentials; the outcome line keeps the one number that teaches (how far off a miss was). A confirmation on the browser's Back: it cannot be held without fighting the browser.

**Consequence.** `.skate__call` is gone (the turn card replaces it). Tests: `src/ui/Skate.test.tsx` (keyboard start and focus, the computer's call before its go, the copy with the guide, the auto turn with fake timers and its holds, the call picker, Slow motion, both leave dialogs, the end), `src/store/skate.test.ts` (the clean start, the leave flow, the auto switch, focus at the end), `src/sim/skate.test.ts` (`turnView`).

## ADR-48: Shoes only by default, quieter heading focus, earlier model downloads, models freed on a lower tier (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Step 1 of `docs/PLAN-LOOK-FEEL-PERF.md`. The owner chose "shoes only" for the rider: the legs (ADR-37) were the weakest visual, and from the overhead rider view (the default in the lab and the game) they cover the board. The heading that receives focus after a screen change drew the control ring round its whole row, which read as a text field. The tier's models only started downloading once the model chunk had arrived. And ADR-15 left a follow-up: a model stays in memory after the student moves to a tier that never shows it.

**Decision.**
- **Legs off by default.** `readLegsVisible` is true only for a saved `on`, so the welcome preview, the game and the lab show the shoes alone until the student switches the Legs switch on in View (it stays, shown while the feet are). A saved `on` still wins. Measured (`scripts/measure`, 2026-11-09, 1440 x 900): High 31 to 28 draw calls in the lab (the two leg meshes and the shanks' shadow pass), Medium 16 to 14, Low 14 to 12; High's GPU time per frame in a kickflip 2.74 to 2.52 ms (median).
- **Heading focus.** The headings a store action focuses (`#screen-heading`, `#setup-heading`, `#skate-heading`, all `tabindex="-1"`) are not controls, so they drop the control ring for a 3 px `--color-focus` underline under the words (`:focus-visible` only, so a mouse user who clicked a button sees nothing). Forced colours keep a system outline. DESIGN.md 14.1.
- **Earlier downloads.** The tier's GLBs (`modelsFor` in modelUrls.ts) start downloading when the TrickScene chunk evaluates (`prefetchModels`, `modelCache.ts`), instead of when the model chunk has arrived and preloads them. The one GLTFLoader is patched once through drei's `extendLoader` hook (`shareEarlyDownloads`, `gltf.ts`): a file started early is parsed from that download, so nothing is fetched twice; a failed early download falls back to the normal load, whose failure still reaches AssetBoundary.
- **Freed on a lower tier (ADR-15 follow-up).** One second after the tier in use drops, TrickScene releases the models the new tier never shows (`modelsUnusedBy`: Medium drops the High board and the shoes, Low all four): the loader's cache entry (`useGLTF.clear`), each geometry, material and texture once, and the decoded `ImageBitmap`s (`close()`). The Canvas is keyed on the tier, so the old context's GPU memory was already gone; what stayed was the cache and its decoded images. The second lets the old R3F root finish tearing down first; a move back up inside it frees nothing.

**Not adopted.** Starting the downloads in `main.tsx` when the page opens, as the plan proposed. Measured at 3 Mbps it brought the models in 0.2 s sooner (9.21 to 9.03 s on High) but pushed the first 3D frame from 1.39 to 3.51 s: the link is bandwidth-bound, so the models only take bytes from the 3D code, and `fetch(..., { priority: 'low' })` did not change that under Chromium's throttling. Starting them as the 3D code arrives keeps the first frame (1.39 s) and still starts the requests 160 ms sooner (9.08 s for every model on High, 3.40 s on Medium instead of 3.51 s). The plan's "about a second sooner" does not exist on such a link; fewer bytes (step 3) is where loading gets faster.

**Consequence.** The scene description has no legs sentence by default (it comes back with the switch). A tier change down and back up re-reads the model from the HTTP cache. `modelCache.test.ts` covers the tier lists, the shared download, the fallbacks, the single patch and the release.

**Update (Phase A review, 2026-11-09).** A model still loading when the student moved down a tier was skipped by the release and stayed cached once it finished. TrickScene now marks the new tier's unused models at once (`setUnwanted`, before the delayed release), and a model that finishes while marked is freed `RELEASE_AFTER_MS` later unless the student has come back to a tier that shows it. The same covers a failure that forces Low.

## ADR-49: KTX2 (Basis Universal ETC1S) textures in every GLB (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Step 3 of `docs/PLAN-LOOK-FEEL-PERF.md`. On High the models were 2.8 MB, almost all of it PNG textures, and they took about 34 MB of GPU memory as RGBA8. The owner approved a build tool to shrink them. BRIEF-BOARD.md had ruled out KTX2 because it seemed to need the native `toktx` binary; `ktx2-encoder` (MIT) runs Basis Universal as WASM in Node, so no system install is needed.

**Pilot (2026-11-09).** Every texture of the four committed GLBs was re-encoded as WebP (quality 85 and 90, normal maps lossless) and as KTX2 (ETC1S at quality 128 and 255, ETC1S with UASTC normal maps, all UASTC), and the variants were served in place of the real files. Totals for High (board, shoes, ground): PNG 2,776 KB and 33.5 MB on the GPU; WebP q90 1,324 KB and 33.5 MB; KTX2 ETC1S q255 966 KB and 8.4 MB; ETC1S with UASTC normals 1,285 KB; all UASTC 3,494 KB. Side-by-side crops at DPR 2 (the grip from the rider view, the deck graphic, a shoe and the concrete from the welcome still) showed no difference between PNG, WebP q90 and ETC1S q255; ETC1S q128 put block steps on the graphic's letters; UASTC normal maps looked the same as ETC1S ones. Time to every model on screen at full speed: PNG 1.05 s, WebP 0.81 s, KTX2 0.77 to 0.82 s.

**Decision.**
- **KTX2, ETC1S, quality 255, with its full mip chain**, for every texture of all four GLBs (`KTX2_OPTIONS` in `scripts/lib/build.ts`). The builder hands the encoder the raw pixels it already makes (no PNG round trip). Colour maps are perceptual with the sRGB transfer function; normal maps use Basis's normal-map preset; roughness stays linear. Basis's mips are in the file, since a compressed texture cannot be mipmapped on the GPU. The build stays deterministic (byte for byte, tested).
- **Runtime.** three's own `KTX2Loader` (`gltf.ts`), its transcoder (`basis_transcoder.js` and `.wasm`, 585 KB, about 260 KB gzipped) served from `public/basis/`: `npm run assets` copies them from the installed three, and `npm run assets:check` fails if they differ from it, so a three upgrade cannot leave a mismatched transcoder. Never a CDN. KTX2Loader picks its GPU format from a renderer, and three-stdlib's GLTFLoader silently drops a texture its KTX2 loader cannot read yet, so the loader parses no model before the first Canvas has noted its renderer (`noteRenderer`, `whenRenderer` in `modelCache.ts`); the download is not held up. On a desktop GPU every texture lands as BC7 (checked in the browser: format 36492, full mip chains, sRGB on colour maps only).
- **The checker** reads the KTX2 header (`ktx-parse`, pinned, already installed through glTF-Transform): real size, and the mip levels stored, which must reach 1 x 1. New rows: every texture KTX2 with `KHR_texture_basisu` required, and full mip chains. GPU memory is estimated at 1 byte a pixel (BC7) plus a third. No limit in `BUDGETS` moved.
- **Dependencies.** `ktx2-encoder` 0.6.0 and `ktx-parse` 1.1.0, both pinned dev dependencies. `scripts/lib/png.ts`, the PNG encoder, is removed with its test: nothing encodes PNG any more.

**Result.** High's models 2,776 to 948 KB on disk (gzipped as GitHub Pages sends them: 2,584 to 754 KB), Medium's 714 to 331 KB (660 to 274 KB); GPU texture memory (estimate) High 33.5 to 8.4 MB, Medium 14.7 to 3.7 MB. Every model on screen at 3 Mbps: High 8.60 to 4.60 s, Medium 3.23 to 3.36 s on a first visit (PERF-BUDGET.md, step 3). The model chunk grows by 24 KB gzipped (KTX2Loader and its zstd decoder), and the transcoder is a one-time 257 KB gzipped (GitHub Pages compresses `.wasm`), cached after the first visit; it starts downloading with the first model request. A transcoder that cannot load fails the model loads, so AssetBoundary shows the simple board and says so. Low downloads none of it.

**Not adopted.** WebP: the smallest first download on Medium (280 KB against 331 KB plus the transcoder), but no GPU memory saving, which is what a mid-range laptop runs out of first, and a larger High download. UASTC: 1.8 to 3.6 times the bytes for no visible gain here. Lower ETC1S quality: block steps on the deck graphic.

**Consequence.** `npm run assets` takes about 13 s instead of under 2 (the encoder is single-threaded WASM). Anything that reads the GLBs' textures must expect `image/ktx2`.

**Update (Phase A review, 2026-11-09).** three-stdlib's GLTFLoader turns a texture that fails to load or decode into a missing map and still reports the model as loaded, so a corrupt KTX2 would have shown an untextured board with nothing said. The loader patch now checks every material slot the file asks a texture for (`missingTextures`, through the parser's material associations) and fails the model when one is empty, disposing the partial result; AssetBoundary then shows the simple board and announces it. Tested with the real GLTFLoader on the committed `board-high.glb`, its KTX2 loads stubbed to succeed and to fail; in the browser, blocking `basis/` gave the simple board and the announcement. The measurement server is now `scripts/measure/serve.ts` (run with `tsx`), on loopback only, and checks that a request stays inside its folder by the resolved path rather than a string prefix (an encoded `..` reached a sibling folder that shared the prefix); `serve.test.ts` covers the escapes.

**Update (PLAN-PLAY-POLISH-REACH step 4, 2026-11-09): one transcoder.** three's `KTX2Loader` names its own transcoder with `new URL('../libs/basis/...', import.meta.url)`, so Vite already emitted a content-hashed copy into `dist/assets/` (584,862 bytes raw) that nobody downloaded, because `gltf.ts` pointed the loader at `public/basis/`. Now the loader keeps its default (no `setTranscoderPath`): it fetches the emitted copy and hands it to its workers. `public/basis/`, the copy step in `npm run assets` and the byte check in `assets:check` are gone. It is still served from this site, never a CDN, and the download is the same (15,469 and 247,919 bytes transferred, checked in a build with `--base=/trick-lab/` on Medium and High, every texture compressed; the dev server serves it from `node_modules`). `dist/` is 584,938 bytes smaller. A three upgrade that changes the transcoder now changes its file name, so no cache can serve a stale one. The download budget counts it from the manifest (`TRANSCODER` in `scripts/lib/bundleBudget.ts`).

## ADR-50: Two renderers: WebGPURenderer on Medium and High, WebGLRenderer on Low (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Step 4 of `docs/PLAN-LOOK-FEEL-PERF.md`. The effects the owner approved (ambient occlusion, motion blur, a grade, step 6) live in three's node renderer: it writes normals and per-object velocity in the scene pass, and three's velocity-driven motion blur exists only for it. three r187 ships `WebGPURenderer` and R3F 9.8.2 accepts an async renderer factory, so no upgrade is needed.

**Decision.**
- **Per tier** (`renderer` in `ART.quality`): Medium and High run `WebGPURenderer`, on WebGPU, or on its own WebGL2 backend where WebGPU is missing. Low keeps `WebGLRenderer`, which starts faster on a Chromebook and is proven there. On Medium and High TrickScene's `gl` is a factory (`rendererFactory`): it loads `src/scene/nodeRenderer.ts`, its own lazy chunk, awaits `init()` (R3F requires it), sets the exposure and registers the TSL materials. If the chunk cannot load or the renderer cannot start, the tier still draws, with `WebGLRenderer` and the GLSL materials, and a console warning (checked in the browser with the chunk blocked). Low keeps today's `gl` props and never downloads the chunk: through the factory, even returning a WebGLRenderer at once, Low's first frame came 0.3 s later in every run (0.45 to 0.76 s at full speed, 1.39 to 1.70 s at 3 Mbps), so the factory is only where it is needed. (WebGLRenderer itself cannot be split out: R3F imports it.)
- **Two shaders, written twice.** The foot trails and the Medium ground shadows were GLSL `ShaderMaterial`s, which the node renderer cannot run. Their TSL twins (`footPathNodeMaterial`, `shadowNodeMaterial`) sit in `nodeRenderer.ts`; both versions read one set of values (`uniforms` in `resources.ts`: the trails' playhead, the board's footprint vectors), which FlightDriver mutates every frame as before (no allocation; the playhead reaches the TSL uniform through `onRenderUpdate`). `shaderMat(key, nodeRenderer)` picks the twin for the renderer in use. The pure parts (`groundShadow.ts`, `footPathGeometry.ts`) are shared and unchanged. Every other material is a standard one, which the node renderer converts.
- **The environment map** uses the node renderer's own `PMREMGenerator` on that path (`prefilterEnvironment`); the warm-up order in SceneEnvironment is unchanged.
- **Counting.** The two renderers count differently (`rendererKind.ts`): WebGLRenderer's `info.render.calls` is the last frame's draw calls; WebGPURenderer's is every render call since it started, its frame's draw calls are `info.render.drawCalls`, and it resets its counts and advances `info.frame` only in its own animation loop, which R3F does not use. PerfProbe, still the one render call, resets the counts before each frame on that path, and `?debug` now also exposes its numbers as `window.__trickLabPerf`, which `scripts/measure` reads on both renderers (GPU time there comes from WebGPU timestamp queries, turned on under `?debug`).
- **Kept.** The fitted PCF shadow camera (ADR-38), zero frames while idle, FlightDriver as the only pose writer, no per-frame allocation.

**Cost.** The node renderer chunk is 170 KB gzipped (612.7 KB minified), above the plan's 100 to 150 KB estimate; three's core moved into a chunk shared by both paths, so Low's download is unchanged (269 KB of 3D code, was 266 KB). The node renderer draws one more call per frame than WebGLRenderer (High 29, was 28; Medium 15, was 14): its output pass, a full-screen draw that tone maps and converts the colour space of its half-float frame buffer.

**Consequence.** A future custom material must be written for both renderers or as a standard material. `renderers.test.ts` covers the tier's renderer, the counting, the shared uniforms and the material choice; the renderers themselves need a GPU and were checked in the browser (PERF-BUDGET.md, steps 4 and 5).

## ADR-51: Post-processing on the WebGPURenderer: occlusion, motion blur, a glow and a grade (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Step 6 of `docs/PLAN-LOOK-FEEL-PERF.md`. The scene draws in about a millisecond of GPU time on High (ADR-50); what it lacks is contact (where things touch), speed (a full-speed flip strobes) and one finished look. The draw-call gates stay 40, 20 and 20, full-screen passes counted.

**Decision.** `src/scene/effects.ts`, in the node renderer's chunk; every number in `ART.post`.
- **One scene pass, several targets.** The scene pass writes colour, packed normals and, on High, per-object velocity in the same draw (MRT), with the renderer's MSAA (four samples). The scene is drawn once.
- **Ambient occlusion**: three's GTAO from that pass's depth and normals, at half resolution on High and Medium (at full resolution High's frame took 7.3 ms of GPU at 2560 x 1440 against 2.8 ms at half, for the same contact shading in side-by-side crops); radius 0.12 m, thickness 0.25 m, 16 samples, mixed in at 0.75. The packed normals are stored at 8 bits a channel, half the bandwidth of the default half-float target. It darkens where shoes meet the grip, round the trucks, under the deck and under the wheels; nothing where there is only a cast shadow (checked by drawing the occlusion alone). Applied in the composite, not in the lighting: three's documented way to apply it to the indirect light only needs a second scene pass, which would double High's draw calls.
- **One composite pass** does the rest: motion blur along the velocity (High, 12 centred taps of equal weight, a 180 degree shutter), the occlusion, a glow round the brightest highlights (High: radiance above 1.1 spreads over two rings of eight taps, 0.4 and 1.1 percent of the stage's height), tone mapping and the colour space (`renderOutput`), then a grade in display space (contrast 1.05, saturation 1.06, shadows a touch cooler and highlights a touch warmer) and a vignette (up to 16 percent at the corners). The plan's "subtle bloom" is this glow: three's BloomNode costs twelve full-screen passes, which would take High past its gate.
- **Motion blur runs only where it shows speed** (`motionBlurOn` in sceneTier.ts): on High, with the switch on, while a trick plays at normal speed. Off in slow motion, paused, scrubbing, before a pop and after the landing, on the welcome preview (slow motion), and under reduced motion. One uniform, set when that changes, never per frame. A "Motion blur" switch in View (shown on High only, where it does something): `motionBlur` in the ui slice, persisted under `trick-lab.motion-blur`, default on, announced ("Motion blur on." or "Motion blur off."), never logged, not exported.
- **Drawing.** `PostEffects` in TrickScene builds the effects once per Canvas on a tier that has them and hands their `render` to PerfProbe (`frame.render` in perfStats.ts), which stays the one render call; it is freed on unmount. Low, and the WebGLRenderer fallback, have none.
- **Draw calls**: High 30 (the scene and its shadow map 28, the occlusion, the composite), Medium 16; the composite replaces the node renderer's own output pass.

**Update (Phase B review, 2026-11-09).** The first build used three r187's `motionBlur` helper and scaled the NDC velocity by 0.5 on both axes. The reviewer found two faults: three's full-screen quad runs its UVs down the screen while NDC runs up, so a diagonal blurred along the mirrored diagonal; and the helper adds one tap more than it divides by, so every still pixel brightened while a trick played (107 to 113 in the browser) and darkened again at the landing. The blur is now the app's own (`blurTaps`): twelve centred taps with equal weights summing to 1, along the velocity times (0.5, -0.5) and the shutter. `effects.test.ts` checks the weights, the centring and the UV convention against three's own quad.

**Not adopted.** Three's BloomNode (twelve passes). TRAA and depth of field (left out by the plan: they smear a spinning board and blur the information). A second scene pass for the occlusion. Denoising the occlusion (another pass; at 0.75 its grain does not show in the final picture).

**Consequence.** Every effect number is in `ART.post`. `effects.test.ts` covers the tiers, when motion blur runs and that the effects build and free without a GPU; the store and UI tests cover the switch. The picture was checked in the browser (PERF-BUDGET.md, step 6).

## ADR-54: Low, designed: toon shading, the real deck, smooth edges, no environment map (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Step 9 of `docs/PLAN-LOOK-FEEL-PERF.md`. The owner chose "simple but finished" for Low: a deliberate flat style, not a broken-looking High. Low was plain grey, a board of boxes, and jagged edges (no antialiasing at DPR 1.5).

**Decision.**
- **Toon shading** (`ART.low`): every Low material is a `MeshToonMaterial` over three steps of light (0.42, 0.72, 1), so a surface is lit, half lit or in shade, in the board's and the shoes' own colours. Medium and the fallback keep standard materials. The sky dome, the trails, the cues and the footprints are unchanged.
- **The primitive board is the real deck** (`BoardPrimitive`, `geo.boardDeck` and `geo.boardHardware` in resources.ts): the Medium GLB's deck mesh (`buildDeck`: outline, kicks, concave) and the trucks (baseplate, yoke, axle housing) and wheels at the GLB's own places (spec.ts), coloured per vertex: two draw calls instead of five, built at the GLB's coordinates, so it rests on the pad with no lift (`primitiveLift` and the old DESIGN.md section 2 box numbers are gone). It is still the Medium and High fallback while a GLB streams, there with one standard material. The sim's board volume keeps the legacy primitive deck it pinned (src/sim/boardVolume.ts, so the feet keep their poses); the new primitive is inside it where the feet reach (tested), and the motion cues still drape by the sim's deck top.
- **Smooth edges**: MSAA at DPR 1 instead of none at DPR 1.5 (`ART.quality.low`).
- **No environment map on Low** (`environment: false`): toon materials do not use one, and its build was the largest single startup task on a throttled Chromebook (ADR-31's PMREM; 188 ms at 4x CPU here). Low also skips the asynchronous warm-up: started with nothing drawn, the parallel shader compile reported ready only about 0.5 s later on this machine (measured), so SceneEnvironment reveals Low at once and its few programs compile in its first frame (one task of 70 to 98 ms at 4x CPU).
- **Slab joints** on the ground plane: a 256 px texture, the slab colour with a joint along each edge (about 2 cm), repeated once per bay, so the board still rests centred in a bay; mipmapped, so far joints fade into the slab.

**Result.** Low draws 10 calls in the lab (was 13; gate 20) and downloads no model; see PERF-BUDGET.md, step 9, for the startup.

**Consequence.** `primitiveLift` is gone from ART; the tests that read it (rest height, clearance, the sim's volume, the flight's contact) now read the built geometry or the asset's numbers.

**Update (Phase C review, 2026-11-09).** Two numbers moved into ART: the primitive yoke's width (`ART.board.primitive.yokeWidth`) and the joint texture's filtering, which now reads Low's `groundAnisotropy`. That was 1 and unused while Low's ground had no texture; it is 4, the value the joints were built and checked with.

## ADR-55: Fog by the distance from the camera (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Review of Phase C (ADR-52). three's Fog fades by view depth, the distance along the camera's axis. A desktop stage has no widest shape: a 2560 x 400 window makes the welcome stage 5.5:1, and there a ray toward the frame's side met the ground plane's edge (20 m out) only 10 m deep, two thirds fogged, a hard line under the haze at both ends of the frame. ADR-31's check stopped at a 2.2:1 stage. Measured the same way, the lab's lowest orbit showed the edge from about 3.6:1 (since ADR-31), and so did the welcome camera once ADR-52 lowered it to 8 degrees.

**Decision.** The fog fades by the distance from the camera (range fog), on both renderers, with the same colour, near and far (`ART.fog`, unchanged), in `src/scene/rangeFog.ts`:
- **WebGLRenderer** (Low, and the fallback): three's four fog chunks, which every built-in material includes (the GLBs' too), hand the view position to the fragment, and the fragment takes its length as the fog's depth; three's own fog factor and mix run unchanged (`rangeFogOnWebGL`, at TrickScene's module load, before any program compiles). Per fragment, not per vertex: the first try wrote the distance per vertex, and since a distance is not linear across a triangle, Low's ground (two triangles with corners 28 m away) came out fully fogged in the browser. If a three upgrade changes a chunk, the edit is skipped with a warning (depth fog) and `rangeFog.test.ts` fails.
- **WebGPURenderer** (Medium, High): `scene.fogNode`, which three reads before `scene.fog`: three's `fog()` with `smoothstep(near, far, positionView.length())`, per fragment, its colour, near and far copied from the scene's Fog once per render, so a theme change is a uniform write and no recompile (`rangeFogNode` in nodeRenderer.ts, put on the scene in TrickScene's `onCreated`).

**Why not a bigger ground, or a cap on the stage's width.** By depth, any plane fails at some width (at 10:1 a 20 m edge is 6 m deep). Capping the horizontal view either crops the flight or backs the camera off until the board is in the fog. By distance, the nearest visible edge from any allowed camera, the welcome one included, is at least 19 m on every stage from 0.5:1 to 50:1, past `far` (15 m). The board's side was already a distance bound (the camera to the target plus the target to the farthest flight point, inside `near`, 5.5 m); see the update below for the one view where it did not hold.

**Consequence.** The ground at the frame's sides fogs a little sooner than at its centre, as a real haze does, and the fog no longer swings as the camera turns. The cost is a length per fragment where three did a negation per vertex. `artDirection.test.ts` and `sky.test.ts` check the edge by distance to 50:1 (the 2560 x 400 case included); `rangeFog.test.ts` checks the chunk edits against this three's chunks and in every built-in material's resolved shaders, and that only a node renderer's scene gets the node. Checked in the browser at 2560 x 400, welcome and the lab's lowest orbit, High, Medium and Low, both themes: the edge line is gone, the ground fades evenly into the haze, no console errors.

**Update (Phase C re-check, 2026-11-09).** Two nits. The near bound held for the rider view only: the detail view on the narrowest lab stage (1024 x 768) let the orbit zoom out to 5.23 m (1.25 times its fit), which put a deck corner 6.0 m from the camera, a trace of fog (0.14 percent), by depth too, since ADR-38's detail view. The orbit's zoom-out now stops where the farthest flight point would reach the fog's `near` (`orbitMaxDistance` in cameraFit.ts, the 1.25 now `ART.camera.zoomOut`): there it zooms out to 4.68 m, still past the fit (4.18 m); on every other stage and view the limit is unchanged. `artDirection.test.ts` checks the bound for both of the student's views and the welcome one on every stage shape. And the node test now walks the fog node's graph: the fade's input is the length of the view position (it fails if that is put back to view depth), and its one per-render callback copies the scene Fog's colour, near and far.

**Context.** Step 10 of `docs/PLAN-LOOK-FEEL-PERF.md`. Loading was the weak spot of the 2026-11-08 review: the stage was a blank box until the 3D code arrived (`Suspense fallback={null}`), then the stand-in board showed until the tier's models came. At 3 Mbps that was a blank stage to 1.4 s on Low and 4.1 s on High, then the stand-in board for another second on High.

**Decision.**
- **The picture**: one still per theme of the real scene, the welcome screen's camera and its still pose (`SHOWCASE_STILL`, what the welcome screen shows under reduced motion and on Low), rendered on High, 1600 x 800, WebP at quality 0.8: 26 KB each (`public/stills/welcome-light.webp`, `welcome-dark.webp`). 2:1 so that on any stage from 0.9:1 to 2:1, where the welcome camera's fit is set by the height, the picture cropped to the stage (`object-fit: cover`) frames the board as the live scene does; on the welcome screen the hand-over is the same view. One picture for every screen and tier, as the plan says: in the lab and the game it cross-fades into the student's view.

Not byte-deterministic (it is a GPU render), so no test compares pixels.
- **In the page**: `StageStill` (`src/ui/StageStill.tsx`) puts the theme's picture over the stage from the first paint, under the stage's overlays, with no pointer events (the orbit works through it), decorative (empty alt, `aria-hidden`; `#scene-description` is the stage's text). It shares the link with the 3D code: the first lit frame comes 50 to 90 ms later than at step 9 (27 KB at 3 Mbps is 72 ms). Draw calls and frame times are unchanged; the picture is DOM. Blocking the ground, the board or the shoes still lifts it (High with the plain ground at 1.8 s; Low at 1.5 s).

**Consequence.** The plan's "no blank box, no blocky stand-in board" holds from the first paint, at the cost of one 26 KB download. The stills are rebuilt by hand (ASSET-PIPELINE.md) when the welcome view changes: a camera, the sky, the light or the board's look; `assets:check` only knows they exist and fit the spec.

## ADR-58: Sound, synthesized and off by default (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Step 12 of `docs/PLAN-LOOK-FEEL-PERF.md`. The owner wants sound, off by default with a switch. Recorded samples would be downloads to license and to fetch past school filters.

**Decision.**
- **Synthesized in the browser** with Web Audio, so nothing is downloaded or licensed: six sounds as a few voices each, a tone gliding between two pitches or a burst of filtered white noise, with a fast attack and an exponential decay (`src/audio/sounds.ts`, pure data): the pop (a woody tock and a low thump: the tail striking), the landing (two clacks, front truck then back, over a thud), the bail (knocks, then a scrape), a letter earned (a sticker slapped on), and a rising and a falling jingle for winning and losing a game. Each lasts under a second; the master gain is 0.6, so they sit under a screen reader's speech.
- **Driven by the store's actions**: `soundCue` (`{ nonce, kind }`, ui slice) is set by the action at the moment the thing happens: `popTrick` (the pop, as the flight starts, the computer's too), the landing's resolution (the landing or the bail, so it comes when the board lands, at the flight's own pace), and the game's scoring (a letter, either player's; then the win or the loss). The store asks whether or not sound is on; nothing is logged or exported.
- **Played by** `useSound` (`src/hooks/useSound.ts`), subscribed to the store outside React's render, so a cue from a click or a key plays inside that user gesture, which browsers need before audio may start. The player (`src/audio/player.ts`) makes the AudioContext on first use and resumes it if suspended; with no Web Audio (jsdom) it does nothing. Each sound is a handful of nodes freed when they end, never in a frame loop. Sounds asked for together (a landing, a letter, the loss) play 0.22 s apart, so each is heard.
- **Slow motion**: the landing already comes when the slowed board lands. The board's own sounds (pop, landing, bail) also come lower and longer below 1x, by speed^0.35, no lower than half pitch (0.25x plays at 0.62 pitch, 1.62 times as long); the game's sounds keep their pitch.
- **The switch**: "Sound", a `.toggle` beside the Light theme switch in the top bar (lab and game) and on the welcome screen. Off by default, saved under `trick-lab.sound`, announced ("Sound on." / "Sound off."), never logged, not exported. Turning it on plays the pop, so the student hears what they turned on. The welcome preview makes no sound: it is not the store's flight.

**Measured.** 3.3 KB more JavaScript in the main bundle (1.5 KB gzipped); no audio work at all while sound is off; in the browser the context runs straight after the switch's click, and at 0.25x the landing's sound came 2.17 s after the pop, as the slowed board landed.

**Update (Phase D review, 2026-11-09).** Turning sound off left what was already queued to play: a loss queues the bail, the letter and the jingle up to 1.2 s ahead. The player now keeps every voice it starts until it ends, and `stop()` (called by `useSound` when sound turns off) cuts them all and resets the stagger. Checked in the browser: a pop and its landing queued 0.22 s ahead, then sound off: all six voices stopped at once. And with sound saved on, the page's first click or key now unlocks the audio, so a first cue from a timer (the computer's own turn in a game) never has to start the audio context outside a gesture, which stricter browsers refuse.

## ADR-59: An automatic step-down when tricks run choppy (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Step 13 of `docs/PLAN-LOOK-FEEL-PERF.md`. The first visit picks a tier from what the browser says about the device (ADR-16), and that guess can be wrong: a machine that looks capable can still draw High's tricks at a stutter. The student should not have to know to open View.

**Decision.**
- **Measured on tricks, not guessed**: while a flight plays, PerfProbe writes each frame's interval into a preallocated array (`trickFrames` in perfStats.ts: one write per frame, no allocation), afresh for each new flight (a pause and resume keep the record). When the flight lands, the landing's resolution asks the scene for that flight's pace (`paceSource`, registered by PerfProbe; `flightPace` in `src/scene/flightPace.ts`): the median interval, leaving out gaps over 250 ms (a hidden tab) and needing at least 12 frames (so a pop resolved at once under reduced motion, or a scrub, is not judged). The median, so a compile hitch or a garbage collection does not count.
- **The store decides** (`noteTrickPace`, `src/store/pace.ts`): only while the tier is the device's own pick (`qualityAuto`), never one the student chose and never after a model failed. The first 3 judged tricks on a tier decide it: if 2 of them ran over 40 ms a frame (under 25 frames a second), the tier drops one step (High to Medium, Medium to Low; Low is the floor), and the new tier is judged afresh. If they ran fine, the tier is kept and no longer judged.
- **Said with the landing**: the step-down is added to the landing's own announcement ("... Tricks were running slowly on this device, so quality is now medium. You can change it in View."), one message, because the live region holds only the latest: the first build announced it separately inside the landing, and the landing's announcement replaced it before a screen reader could read it. Never logged, not exported.
- **Remembered**: the tier stepped to is saved as `trick-lab.quality-step`, and later visits cap the device's pick at it (`capQuality`), so the next visit starts on the tier that ran. A tier the student picks in View wins, as before (ADR-16), and moves back up.

**Checked.** In the browser on the RTX 3060 (High by the device's pick): three kickflips ran smoothly and kept High. With 45 ms of main-thread work forced into every animation frame (about 20 frames a second), the third trick's announcement carried the step-down, the scene moved to Medium, and the next visit downloaded `board.glb` and showed Medium in View. CPU throttling alone (20x) did not trigger it: this scene's per-frame CPU work is about 0.6 ms, so tricks stayed smooth, which is right. The measurement script always sets a tier (`m-quality`), so it is never stepped down.

**Update (Phase D review, 2026-11-09).** The 12-frame minimum missed the very devices this is for: a normal-speed kickflip lasts 0.53 s, so at 20 frames a second it records about 10 intervals and was never judged (the reviewer reproduced it at 20, 15 and 10 frames a second, and in Chromium with 65 ms of work per frame). A trick is now judged by the time its frames cover: at least 250 ms in at least 2 intervals. The gaps the 250 ms cut-off guarded against now never enter the record: the first frame of a run of playing (after a pop, a resume or a hidden tab, `recordFrame` in flightPace.ts) is left out, since it spans the time before it; only one-off stalls over a second are dropped. `flightPace.test.ts` drives the real store and flight clock through three kickflips at 60, 30, 20, 15, 10 and 5 frames a second, recording each frame as PerfProbe does: High steps down at 20 and below and is kept at 30 and 60. In Chromium with 65 ms of work per frame, the third trick now steps High to Medium.

**Update (Phase D re-check, 2026-11-09).** Two more gaps. A pause, a scrub and a resume between two drawn frames left the run of frames unbroken, so the next frame's interval spanned the whole pause (800 ms in the reviewer's case) and three such flights stepped a smooth device down: any playback change in the store (status or flight) now ends the run (`noteFramesStoreChange`, subscribed by PerfProbe, as the flight clock's `noteStoreChange` is), so that frame is never recorded. And the 250 ms minimum per flight left short flights unjudged for good (a 0.15 m pop at 2x covers about 150 ms at 20 frames a second, one interval at 10): the record is now a pool that short flights add to, judged at the landing that brings it to 250 ms and emptied then, so a sample is one trick or several short ones (emptied too when a Canvas, a tier, mounts). `flightPace.test.ts` drives the real store and flight clock through these: High steps down within a dozen tricks for a 0.15 m pop at 2x at 20, 15 and 10 frames a second and a kickflip at 2x at 10, stays at 30 and 60, and a pause, scrub and resume between frames on every flight at 60 never steps it down (both tests fail without the fix).

**Update (second Phase D re-check, 2026-11-09).** Stalls over a second were stored in the pool and only left out when judging, so long stalls (seven 0.6 m flights at 0.05x with 1.25 s frames) could fill all 900 places with nothing judgeable and stop the step-down until the Canvas remounted. `recordFrame` now never stores an interval the judging would leave out, and a full pool that still cannot be judged is emptied. `flightPace.test.ts` fills the pool that way, then a 15 fps stretch at 1x steps High down (it failed before the fix).

## ADR-60: The legs in one draw, faded by a dither (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Step 2 of `docs/PLAN-PLAY-POLISH-REACH.md`. Medium's gate is under 20 draw calls, and with every switch on (feet, legs, foot trails, motion cues) it drew 20 in a trick (measured 2026-11-09, PERF-BUDGET.md, "Play, polish and reach plan"). The legs were two skinned meshes (ADR-37): the shanks opaque, the thighs in a second, transparent material (vertex alpha, no depth write) that fades them toward the hips.

**Decision.**
- **One mesh, one opaque material.** Both legs are one geometry (`geo.legs`, resources.ts) over the same four-bone skeleton. The thighs' fade is the same vertex alpha, now drawn as a screen-door dither: the fragment is dropped when its alpha is at or below a 4 x 4 ordered (Bayer) threshold of its pixel. Opaque, so the legs write depth and need no sorting.
- **Twice, as rule 7 asks.** GLSL for WebGLRenderer: the standard material with the dither patched in after its alpha test (`legsMaterial`, `DITHER_GLSL`, its own program cache key). TSL for the WebGPURenderer: a standard node material with the threshold as its `alphaTestNode` (`legsNodeMaterial`, nodeRenderer.ts). One formula for both; `ditherThreshold` is its JavaScript copy, tested to give each of the 16 thresholds once per block.
- **The shadow stays the shanks'.** A vertex attribute `aCast` marks the shanks. The TSL twin masks the shadow pass with it (`maskShadowNode`); on WebGLRenderer (High's fallback) the mesh's `customDepthMaterial` drops the thighs. As before, the thighs cast nothing (their shadow would be the whole thigh).

**Consequence.** One draw call fewer on every tier with the legs on: every switch on is now High 34, Medium 19, Low 12 (measured, step 1's script). Checked against the two-mesh build in six poses of a kickflip on every tier: the same legs. One visible difference, for the better: on Medium and High the occlusion pass drew a faint grey disc around the transparent thighs near the hips (they wrote no depth, so the effects saw the ground through them); the dithered thighs write depth only where they show, and the disc is gone. The fade itself is visibly different: a fine dot pattern just above each knee from the rider's view, and from the low detail camera a halftone over the whole fading part of each thigh, where the old fade was smooth. That is the price of the draw call; the legs are off by default (ADR-48).

## ADR-61: Browser checks in CI, with Playwright as a pinned dev dependency (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Step 5 of `docs/PLAN-PLAY-POLISH-REACH.md`. Until now everything a browser can show (a screen that fails to start, a draw call too many, a frame drawn after a landing, memory that grows) was checked by hand through the global `playwright-cli`, and `BRIEF.md` and `CLAUDE.md` forbade Playwright in the project. The plan's later steps change the 3D view and the game in every commit, and a hand check does not run on a pull request. The owner said yes to Playwright as a pinned dev dependency for this (2026-11-09).

**Decision.**
- **`@playwright/test` 1.64.1, pinned, Chromium only**, as `npm run test:browser` (`playwright.config.ts`, `browser/`). It runs against the production build `npm run verify` ends with, served by `scripts/measure/serve.ts` the way GitHub Pages sends it. It is not part of `verify`, which stays quick and needs no browser.
- **No GPU, on purpose.** Chromium runs with SwiftShader, so a laptop, this desktop and a CI machine draw the same way. WebGPU is hidden, so Medium and High run the WebGPURenderer on its WebGL2 backend and Low the WebGLRenderer, and every check asserts the renderer it got: a fallback cannot pass as High.
- **What is checked** (through `?debug`: the R3F root, PerfProbe's numbers, the store):
  - `screens.spec.ts`: every screen on every tier starts with no console error;
  - `ledger.spec.ts`: every switch combination's draw calls, at rest and over a 360 flip, against the tier's cap and its expected worst case;
  - `landing.spec.ts`: a landing draws at most the one frame its own write queued, then none (rule 4);
  - `soak.spec.ts`, the long-run test (ROADMAP "later" item 9): 5 warm-up tricks, then 50 in blocks of ten, switching between High and Low before each block.
- **The long-run test's hard check is a count, its heap check is loose.** The geometry and texture counts at a tier must equal that tier's first sample exactly: that cannot fail by chance, and it is what a leaked model or map moves. The heap may grow 4 MB from the first High sample to the last, about twice what was measured, because the heap moves with V8's compiled code (about 1 MB over four tier changes) and the machine. A snapshot of the heap without compiled code is taken with each sample and attached to the report, not asserted.
- **In CI** (`.github/workflows/verify.yml`): `verify` uploads its build, and two more jobs run on it: the quick checks, and the long-run test alone (tagged `@soak`), so a slow run does not hold up the others.
- **`playwright-cli` stays** for looking at the app by hand, the measurement and the loading pictures. Playwright in the project is for these checks only: never upgraded, and never given another browser, without asking.

**A leak it found.** The shared materials in `resources.ts` were freed only when `TrickScene` unmounted, although rule 7 said "when the Canvas unmounts", and a tier change remounts only the Canvas. Each renderer that draws a shared material adds a dispose listener to it, and the listener holds that renderer's program cache (the heap snapshot's retainer path: the material's `_listeners.dispose`, a closure, the program cache). So every tier change kept the last renderer's programs alive. Fixed in 9b7c140: `SharedResources`, inside the Canvas, frees them when that Canvas unmounts, and the TSL twins are freed only by the renderer that registered them, since the next Canvas's renderer can register its own before the old Canvas has unmounted. Rule 7 now says so. Over the long run the heap without compiled code grew 530 KB before the fix and 260 KB after (PERF-BUDGET.md, step 5).

**Consequence.** Any step that touches the 3D view or the game runs `npm run test:browser` as well as `verify`. The checks take about 4 minutes here. They prove counts and frames, not the picture: a visual change is still looked at in a real browser with a GPU, on every tier. The first run in CI happens when the branch is pushed; until then the CI jobs are unproven.

## ADR-62: Good hardware is the design target: frame-time targets replace the tight draw-call caps, and Low is frozen (2026-11-09)

**Context.** Step 6 of `docs/PLAN-PLAY-POLISH-REACH.md`. The owner changed the project's aim on 2026-11-09: the lab, students and Chromebooks matter less, and the work goes into the game of SKATE, the assets and graphics, performance, and how the game feels. Until now every tier but High had to stay under 20 draw calls (the original Chromebook budget, ADR-16), and ROADMAP.md's ground rule gave every new visual feature an off switch that Low used. Medium drew 19 of its 20, so the plan's rolling wheels, skate spot and dust could not be added to it. No check was failing: the device the app is designed for changed.

**Decision.**
- **High is what the game is designed for:** a desktop or laptop with a real graphics chip. **Medium** is for a laptop with built-in graphics and gets every new feature except real shadows, motion blur and glow. **Low is frozen:** a simple fallback for old or weak machines that keeps its look and its cap, and gets no new feature except rolling (a sliding texture costs almost nothing).
- **The question is "does it stay smooth", measured as time per frame**, on the reference machine (the owner's desktop, an RTX 3060, Chromium, 1440 x 900, the build served by `scripts/measure/serve.ts`):

  | | High | Medium | Low |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Graphics time per frame, in a trick and while rolling | under 4 ms | under 3 ms | under 2 ms |
  | The app's own work per frame | under 4 ms | under 4 ms | under 4 ms |
  | Longest single frame, after the first second on screen | 16.7 ms | 16.7 ms | 16.7 ms |
  | Draw calls, every switch on | under 60 | under 40 | under 20, unchanged |

- **How each is read.** `scripts/measure/measure.js` now reports, over the lab's timed tricks, the median, the 95th percentile and the longest of: the graphics time (a GPU timer query), the app's own work (`perfStats.workMs`: from R3F's before-effect to the end of the draw, so FlightDriver's pose and the render call together; one more `performance.now()` a frame), and the interval between frames. Its last table checks the 95th percentile of the first two and the longest interval against the targets and says pass or fail. The longest-frame gate is under 20 ms: 16.7 ms plus the timer's jitter. On a 60 Hz display that fails on any missed refresh (33.3 ms); on a 120 Hz one, as the reference machine's browser runs, one missed refresh (16.7 ms) passes and two (25 ms) fail.
- **Low's graphics target is under 2 ms.** The plan said "as today (about 1 ms)". Today's Low measures 0.2 to 1.0 ms depending on the run, and GPU timer results move by 0.3 ms or more between runs of one build (PERF-BUDGET.md, "How to measure"), so a 1 ms gate would fail by chance. 2 ms still fails if Low doubles.
- **Draw calls stay a gate, with room.** The browser checks enforce the caps (`GATE` in `browser/helpers.ts`: 60, 40, 20) and the expected worst case with every switch on (`LEDGER`: 34, 19, 12 today), which moves only in the commit of a step that adds or removes a draw.
- **Downloads keep their budget** (`npm run bundle:check`): the target device changed, not the network.
- **The ground rule changes.** "Every new visual feature gets an off switch, and the Low setting uses it" becomes: Low does not get new features, and a new switch is added only where a player would want one.

**Measured** (2026-11-09, this step's build, feet on, dark theme; the whole table is in PERF-BUDGET.md, "Step 6"): High 1.05 ms of graphics time, 1.4 ms of work, longest interval 16.6 ms, 34 draw calls; Medium 0.66 ms, 1.0 ms, 8.6 ms, 19; Low 0.2 ms, 0.7 ms, 8.5 ms, 12. Every tier passes.

**Consequence.**
- Medium has room for 20 more draw calls and High for 25, which the plan's steps 12, 14 and 19 use 3 and 4 of.
- **Medium's target is a stand-in.** It is measured on the reference machine with Medium chosen, not on built-in graphics. One game on a real laptop (step 20) is worth more than the number.
- The automatic step-down (ADR-59) still protects a device that cannot keep up: it judges real tricks at 40 ms a frame, far above these targets.
- CLAUDE.md's rule 14 and verify loop, BRIEF.md's opening, ROADMAP.md's ground rule and PERF-BUDGET.md's targets say the same from this commit.
