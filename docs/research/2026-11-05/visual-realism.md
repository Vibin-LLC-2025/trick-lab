# Visual realism research for trick-lab

Research date: 2026-11-05. Project read: `C:\dev\trick-lab`. Report only. No project files were modified, no packages were installed, and no agents were spawned.

## Scope, evidence and recommendation

The highest-return work is better shoe silhouettes on Medium, coherent wear and roughness variation, physically coherent truck construction, and shadows that explain the board's height. Keep the existing WebGL renderer for this pass. The project already has a procedural outdoor PMREM, a directional key, textured concrete, tiered assets, High shadows and demand rendering. Replacing those systems before correcting the assets is unlikely to be the best use of the budget. This is a project-specific recommendation, not a benchmark result. [P1-P5, S12-S18]

Citation convention: each bracketed source ID resolves to a record in section 7 giving title, outlet, publication/update date, URL and reading scope. `P` identifies the local project, `S` an external source, and `R` a photographic/video reference in section 6. All sources were accessed on 2026-11-05. An undated page is explicitly identified as undated; access dates are not passed off as publication dates. Calculations and proposed values are labelled. Cost and visual-gain judgments are engineering estimates requiring device measurement, not sourced GPU timings. Reference videos were identified through their pages and metadata, not watched frame by frame in this research pass.

## 2. Material appearance and physically based values

### Interpret the numbers correctly

Albedo is reflectance without lighting, not a sampled screen color. Base-color textures are sRGB; roughness, normal, metallic and occlusion data are linear/non-color. The Physically Based v2 database labels its RGB entries `srgb-linear`. Its Aluminum entry is approximately **[0.916, 0.923, 0.924] linear, metalness 1**, roughly sRGB 245-246. Its Concrete preset is **[0.51, 0.51, 0.51] linear, roughness 0.5, IOR 1.5**. These are specific presets, not laws for every dirty concrete or cast alloy. The database's mirror-metal roughness 0 must not be mistaken for a measured used-truck finish. Adobe's guidance puts most dielectric base colors around 30-240 in sRGB and treats metalness as a material mask, with mixed values justified at dirt/coating transitions. [S10, S11]

I found no calibrated database entry for this exact grip tape, maple lacquer, wheel compound or suede shoe. The ranges below are **proposed authoring ranges**, bounded by those published PBR conventions and the visual references. They must not be presented as measured values of these exact objects. ambientCG Concrete 048 is a photogrammetric reference and Poly Haven Concrete Floor 02 supplies separate diffuse, roughness, normal and displacement maps; neither landing page publishes a universal roughness interval for all concrete. [S10, S11, S19, S20]

### What to implement within the existing texture gates

1. **Grip:** currently Medium's grain and glints are albedo only; High adds a normal map. Baked bright pixels cannot produce true directional sparkle. First use filtered normal grain and roughness variation at flick zones, with larger dust/rubber smears in deck coordinates. Isotropic random grit is the default physical assumption. Coherent anisotropy is more appropriate for brushed metal or repeated abrasion than for an entire abrasive sheet. A true sparse, view-dependent glint model needs custom shader work. Texture anisotropic filtering in `ART.quality.anisotropy` is unrelated to BRDF anisotropy. [P3-P4, S11, S13, S21; recommendation]
2. **Deck:** bake original graphic over subtle wood variation, with slide scratches, exposed wood and a shared wear mask. Razor tail and chips that change silhouette need geometry; a normal map cannot remove a rail. Avoid uniformly covering the deck with noise. Use a fixed seed and a mild wear state so nose/tail teaching markers stay legible. [P1, P4, S1, S10; recommendation]
3. **Trucks:** add UVs or bake vertex variation before adding texture maps. `hardware.ts` deliberately removes UVs, so merely assigning a scratch texture will not work. A procedural UV-free shader is another route, but increases maintenance. Keep most cast areas rough and selected rubbing/grinding areas smoother. [P4, S10-S11; recommendation]
4. **Wheels:** current band atlas has no variation around circumference. Its polar lathe UVs need an appropriate radial/side-face atlas for a readable wheel print. Preserve bearing recess geometry and use the existing steel group for genuinely metallic bearing surfaces if rebatching is practical; current wheel-atlas races use a dielectric wheel material. [P4; recommendation]
5. **Ground:** normal, roughness and albedo already contain macro noise, aggregate, pits, cracks, joints, wheel marks and paint. The main issue is repeated identical bays, including identical cracks and the center mark. Vary bays during deterministic baking or subdivide the 2-triangle ground within its 64-triangle limit and use vertex color for low-frequency variation. A larger unique atlas must trade texel density against uniqueness. Keep joints aligned, avoid mirroring normal maps without correcting tangent orientation, and avoid expensive triplanar sampling as the default. [P3-P4, S19-S20; recommendation]

Medium board already uses all four allowed textures. Shoes already use both, and ground all three; High board has five of six. Extra maps are not free additions. Repack existing textures, replace a less useful map, use vertex colors, or write a narrowly justified pipeline/ADR change. glTF metallic-roughness uses G for roughness and B for metallic; AO can share R only with correct per-slot UV/sampling treatment. A normal map is not safely interchangeable with an sRGB color atlas. Avoid shader-specific channel tricks before checking byte, texture and loader gates. [P1, P4, P7, S10, S25]

Avoid mutating a shared cached material according to whichever tier mounted last. [P1, P7-P8, S25]

## 3. Skate shoes and the minimum rider context

Vans' Skate Old Skool reference is a vulcanized low top with suede/canvas panels, padded collar, structured heel, tongue straps, rubber reinforcement under high-wear regions and a waffle sole. A construction reference specifies an Old Skool suede vamp around **1.3 mm** and flat braided laces **10 x 1.5 mm**, plus separate layered foxing wraps. Those are useful real construction details rather than an assumption that every part is one smooth surface. [S22-S23]

Project shoes are 285 x 105 mm with a 24 mm sole envelope, a smoothly lofted upper, painted eyelets and panel colors, and cylindrical laces of radius 2.2 mm, or 4.4 mm diameter. High draws the shoe GLB twice; Medium and Low draw a sole box and upper box per foot. This means improving `shoe.ts` alone changes High, not Medium. [P4-P5]

| Priority shape cue | Recommended procedural treatment | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sole versus foxing | Separate the visible sidewall height from the actual underfoot rubber thickness; retain 24 mm initially as an envelope | A 24 mm visible wrap does not prove a 24 mm slab under the foot. The wear test describes a thin vulcanized outsole and a cushioning insole. Proposed modelling starts: roughly 4-7 mm outsole and 18-25 mm visible foxing height, then fit a chosen shoe. These numeric ranges are estimates, not verified Vans dimensions. [P4, S22-S24] |
| Toe cap and toe spring | Round asymmetrically, add a distinct foxing/toe bumper and slight upward toe spring | Start with a proposed 3-6 mm toe rise; compare profiles. Avoid turning the shoe into a bulbous running shoe. [S23; proposal] |
| Collar and tongue | Give the ankle opening an actual rim/inner recess and make the tongue a separate overlapping shape | Current valley in a continuous loft risks reading as a sculpted groove rather than an opening. Model 2-4 mm panel overlap only where visible. [P4, S22-S23; proposal] |
| Laces | Flatten the cross section to a ribbon/rounded rectangle; add a modest knot and two short ends | 10 x 1.5 mm construction reference contrasts with the current 4.4 mm round tubes. Six crossings can remain; do not model fibers. [P4, S23] |
| Eyelets, seams and panels | Small geometry for holes/rims that affect highlights; atlas/vertex cues for stitching and panel boundary | Do not allocate a draw call for every eyelet or seam. Keep upper and lace batching. [P1, P4, S22-S23; recommendation] |
| Suede and wear | Nonperiodic fine nap, broad worn patch at the lateral forefoot, scuffed foxing, subtle creases | A wear hole is one possible later state, not mandatory. The Skate Classics test shows wear reaching reinforced underlayers and also substantial sole/foxing wear. Use a dark backing in a worn patch, not a transparent alpha hole by default. [S22, S24] |
| Sole underside | Atlas waffle/check tread, with shallow geometry only on High close views | Needed during a flick when the sole turns toward the viewer; not a 30,000-triangle waffle extrusion. [S22-S23; recommendation] |

At approximately 0.3 m physical shoe length, screen pixels are the important second scale. For illustration, a 300-pixel-long shoe has about 1 mm per pixel: a 10 mm lace is about 10 pixels, while a 1.3 mm panel edge is approximately 1 pixel. At 150 pixels those become 5 and 0.65 pixels. These are calculated examples, not screenshots of this app. Spend triangles on footprint, collar, tongue, toe bumper and flat lace highlights. Fine stitches belong in filtered texture/normal detail. Adding more 72 x 48 loft tessellation will not create missing construction. [P4, S23; calculated recommendation]

For Medium, either make a lighter merged procedural shoe silhouette using the existing two materials/calls per foot, or deliberately adopt a reduced shoe GLB. The first option maintains current loading behavior and total calls. If reusing the existing three-call GLB twice, Medium's documented 15 calls would become approximately 17 with the same scene, before other additions. That fits under 20 arithmetically, but raises download, triangle, memory and shader work and requires laptop testing. It also changes the documented tier contract. [P1-P5; calculated proposal]

### Are ankles and lower legs necessary?

They are not necessary for explaining a foot trajectory, but a real-shoe appearance with empty collars still reads as detached objects. For an optional realism view, a short sock, ankle and lower shin are likely a larger perceptual improvement than micro-texture. This is an art-direction inference, not a measured perception-study result. Keep feet-only as a useful teaching mode. Avoid a severed shin cap in the middle of the frame; crop naturally or continue the shape out of view. [P3-P5, S22-S24, R6-R7; recommendation]

Minimum useful rig: per side, a lower-leg root, an ankle joint controlling foot pitch/roll/yaw, and optionally a forefoot/toe joint for sole bend. A rigid shoe plus tapered sock/shin mesh can be done without skinning, with 1-2 material groups merged across both sides where practical. Two or three bones per side suffice for a small skinned alternative. If knee positions are shown or the view can orbit widely, add a knee and hip target or two-bone leg IK, rather than rigidly rotating a shin with the shoe. Preserve the feet model as the contact authority; derive the presentation joints from its poses inside `FlightDriver`, without introducing a second pose-writing `useFrame`. Bone counts are this report's implementation recommendation, not an anatomy claim. [P1, P5; recommendation]

Keep leg additions inside the scene call caps and existing asset gates, or introduce a separately justified rider asset contract. Do not silently exceed the shoe's 3-call and 2-texture limits by treating legs as invisible extras. High already has about 29 documented calls, including shadow draws, so additional cast-shadow materials can consume headroom twice. [P1-P3; recommendation]

WebGPURenderer is available with automatic WebGL2 backend fallback and TSL/node materials, but the official manual still calls it experimental. Fiber v9 supports an async `gl` factory that constructs the renderer and awaits `init()`. That does not make every drei helper or custom GLSL shader portable. In this app the outdoor environment and foot-trail shader are different cases: the sky's Basic materials can be rendered, while custom `ShaderMaterial` effects require porting. The current `PMREMGenerator` import and renderer types also need review. Existing GLSL `onBeforeCompile`, RawShaderMaterial and old EffectComposer passes are outside the new-renderer contract. [S12, S16, P3, P5, P8]

A WebGPU experiment would therefore require an isolated compatibility pass: async initialization, appropriate `three/webgpu` and `three/tsl` imports, TSL replacements for custom scene shaders, renderer-specific PMREM creation, a node render pipeline for post effects, feature-gated helpers, cache/disposal checks and demand-loop tests. All three tiers need fallback verification on actual browsers/devices. Do not estimate a speedup from the API name. Keep r187/Fiber 9/drei 10 for the first realism pass. [P1, S12, S16; recommendation]

### Specific lighting corrections worth testing

The shadow normal bias is **0.015 m**, or **15 mm**. That exceeds the deck's 11.5 mm thickness. This is a suspicious scale for wheel/sole contact, even though offset direction and slope matter and the value alone does not prove visible peter-panning. Trial a lower normal bias around 1-3 mm, fit the shadow frustum tightly to all flight casters, and check acne versus detached contact in rest, edge-on flips and low orbits. Keep bias settings in `artDirection.ts`. The proposed range is an experiment, not a universal three.js setting. [P3, P5, P8; recommendation]

The environment's sun has angular radius **0.05 radians**, approximately **2.86 degrees**, or a 5.73-degree diameter before the additional PMREM blur of 0.04 radians. The real Sun is about half a degree in diameter. This is a deliberately broad highlight source, not a physical solar disc. Either keep it and describe the lighting as diffuse/hazy, or test a roughly 0.0045-0.0047-radian solar radius with a corresponding energy/exposure rebalance. Do not shrink the disc at the same radiance and assume the integrated lighting stays the same. Matching reflection width, key-light direction and shadow softness matters more than attaching the word physical to the renderer. [P3, S42; calculated recommendation]

The sky PMREM, hemisphere and direct key all contribute fill/illumination. Their current sum is an art-direction choice. Reduce duplicate fill only through a side-by-side reference test, not by removing all hemisphere light on principle. Maintain readable graphic/grip alternation and the existing accessible DOM description. [P1, P3, S17; recommendation]

### KTX2 without a CDN: feasible now, but decoding and encoding are separate

The installed three package already contains `examples/jsm/libs/basis/basis_transcoder.js` and `basis_transcoder.wasm`, approximately **57,529 and 527,333 bytes**, respectively. They can be copied through a build step into a same-origin directory such as `public/decoders/basis/`; `KTX2Loader.setTranscoderPath('/decoders/basis/')` and `detectSupport(renderer)` configure the runtime. They are not present in the current public assets merely because the dependency contains them. Keep the JS/WASM pair version-matched and include them in any offline/precache plan. No CDN is technically required and no renderer upgrade is required. [P8, S39]

`useGLTF(url, false, true, extendLoader)` can set `loader.setKTX2Loader(ktx2Loader)` via the fourth argument. In this exact drei version the GLTFLoader comes from **three-stdlib**, not the current three addon; local source confirms the extension hook and Basis support. Configure preload with the same loader setup and avoid a module-scope KTX2 preload before renderer capabilities are known. Review shared loader lifetime on tier remounts and cache keys if separate renderer-capability variants are introduced. [P8, S25, S39]

On the build side, `KHR_texture_basisu` permits KTX2 payloads in the GLB but does not encode PNGs automatically. The glTF Transform CLI exposes ETC1S/UASTC commands, while the current project has the core/extensions/functions libraries and no CLI-based KTX stage. An approved pinned native KTX/Basis encoder or separately evaluated encoder integration is still required. Existing `BRIEF-BOARD.md` explicitly defers native tooling installation. Nothing was installed in this task. Do not claim that bundling the runtime transcoder also solves deterministic texture encoding. [P2, P6-P7, S40-S41]

Suggested future workflow, not run here: pin encoder version and flags, generate PNG source maps, encode color with a quality-tested ETC1S or UASTC choice, use UASTC first for normals and packed material maps, embed as `image/ktx2` with required `KHR_texture_basisu`, and retain meshopt independently for geometry. Validate color-space metadata, mips, sampler/wrap modes, decoder loading, worker behavior and cross-device quality. Existing PNG-specific checker/decoder paths need to inspect KTX2 dimensions and mip chains without weakening the existing limits. The glTF Transform docs warn about ETC1S artifacts on RGB normals and packed material maps and do not promise reduced download size. [P1, P7, S40-S41; recommendation]

Do not budget only GLB bytes. KTX2 moves some work into transcoding and adds same-origin decoder assets. Compare cold-load startup, upload stall, GPU memory and image quality, with the most promising first candidate being the current 11 MiB ground maps on Medium. [P2-P3, S39-S41; recommendation]

## 5. Motion realism for the picture

**First improve shadows and framing. Add blur only after those are convincing.** This is the report's prioritization for this fixed-camera teaching app. [P3-P5, S33-S34, S43-S44; recommendation]

### Board and shoe shadows during flight

High already casts shadows from board and shoes. Improve their near-contact behavior and filtering rather than adding a second dark blob. Medium/Low have one board-centered ellipse which stays at X/Z zero, fades with board height, shrinks from 1.0 to 0.8, follows yaw and changes aspect using pitch/roll. Shoes have no individual footprint shadows on these tiers. [P3, P5]

For Medium/Low, project the board center and shoe soles along the same directional-light ray used by High: for point `(x,y,z)` and light-to-scene direction `d` with negative `d.y`, ground projection is `(x - y*d.x/d.y, 0, z - y*d.z/d.y)`. This is an analytic geometry derivation, not a new physics model. Reuse preallocated matrices/vectors in `FlightDriver`. Fade and broaden the soft component with separation, keeping a contact component only near the ground. A fixed shrink-to-0.8 ellipse is not a general model of a sun's increasing penumbra. [P3, P5; derived recommendation]

Add two soft shoe footprints using one shared material and batched/instanced geometry if practical, rather than two separate material systems. One additional call is a reasonable design target, not a measured result. Draw the shadows only when feet are visible, preserve no depth writes, and update on timeline scrub as well as playback. Local overlap can become too dark; tune union/opacity or accept a mild stylized cue instead of multiplying three dense black ellipses. This should provide a strong height/contact cue on cheaper tiers without a shadow-map scene pass. [P1, P3, P5; recommendation]

### Blur: technically possible, low priority

A camera-motion-only reconstruction does little for a stationary camera and rapidly rotating board. Per-object velocity needs previous and current object transforms, correct projection and motion discontinuity handling. Three's `VelocityNode` tracks previous model/view/projection matrices, and the TSL `motionBlur` helper consumes motion vectors with a sample count, default 16. That is on the new node pipeline, not a drop-in EffectComposer effect for the current app. [S43-S44, S16]

A WebGL implementation would need a velocity pass or custom material pipeline plus a blur gather, with silhouette bleeding/disocclusion handling. Extra scene rendering can violate the draw-call caps even if the blur pass itself is one triangle. A fast flip also has curved rotational trajectories which a single linear motion vector only approximates over the shutter interval. This is a rendering-method limitation and implementation estimate, not an observed defect in a built feature. [S43-S44, P2; inference]

If eventually prototyped on High, use a short proposed exposure such as 1/120 to 1/240 second at normal speed, cap blur length, and turn it off for pause, scrub, reduced motion, catch inspection and slow teaching playback. Clear history on resets, preset/stance changes and camera jumps. Scale from actual display motion and playback speed, not a stored 10 Hz progress commit. Do not add translucent ghost boards as a default: they consume calls, complicate transparent sorting and obscure which way the deck faces. These shutter values and policies are recommendations, not measured camera parameters for a particular reference video. [P1, P5, S43-S44; recommendation]

### Camera and picture composition

The current 36-degree camera, 60-degree-elevated rider view and flight-volume fit are already intentional teaching choices. Keep them as the default. Add an optional low three-quarter detail view for realism evaluation, not an automatic camera move during the trick. A proposed 30-40-degree elevation makes deck thickness, truck mass and sole separation easier to judge. Keep enough ground/shadow in view, avoid clipping raised toes or any added shin, and keep the nose marker legible. These angles are proposed art direction, not photographic measurements. [P3, P5, R1, R6-R8; recommendation]

Maintain the same rest, rising, edge-on, inverted, catch and landed poses when comparing material changes, with trails/cues off for a realism review and also on for the teaching review. Avoid depth of field or camera shake: the small deck and shoes are the information. If adding limbs, expand the camera fit envelope before choosing a crop. Framing is effectively free in steady-state GPU work; it can raise perceived detail simply by allocating more pixels to the subject. [P1, P3, P5; recommendation]
